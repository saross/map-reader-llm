## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Ekim, Sertel and Kabadayı segment five road types from the Deutsche Heereskarte 1:200,000 Türkei (DHK 200 Turkey), a Second World War German military series, using U-Net++ and ResneXt50_32x4d with ImageNet-pretrained weights. In the historical-map extraction lineage it is the closest substrate analogue to ours: a standardised military series at fixed scale, a legend repeated across sheets, and a target read off cartographic convention, not off the ground. In task shape it is the rung below ours on our difficulty ladder — dense pixel-wise multi-class segmentation of continuous linear features, six classes counting background, on 256 × 256 tiles (18,761 cut, 11,685 discarded, the rest split 4953/1415/708) — not localisation of a point symbol.

Metric reporting is the first lesson. The abstract calls "98.73% overall accuracy, 41.99% intersection of union, and 46.61% F1 score" remarkable results; §4 then concedes that accuracy "provided optimistic value", with precision 37.94 and recall 58.78 (Table 1, page_index 11). Their position is symmetric, though: the same paragraph holds that "the remaining scores tended to be overly critical", and blames the spread on the annotation strategy — road thickness, imperfect overlap with the map line, and annotation-broken road segments — not on class imbalance. On our reading roads are thin marks on a background-dominated raster, so pixel accuracy mostly counts empty pixels — the aggregate-versus-relevant-class trap, and a reason to distrust any accuracy figure from this lineage without its F1 beside it. Class-wise, their reading of the confusion matrix (96.4% inferior roads, 99.7% stabilized roads) as near-equal competence sits against Figure 10's trouble with underrepresented classes; on the usual convention those diagonals are recall, not precision — our inference, since the paper does not state its normalisation.

Ground-truth epistemics are the second lesson. Where map markers occlude a road, the annotators digitised it through anyway, so the mask asserts what the image does not show and the model is penalised for not inventing it. Their remedy — a hand-picked non-occluded subset of 300 of 708 test patches, argued representative only from its size — is the curator-versus-adjudicated question in miniature, and its Table 2 column does not cohere as we have it: F1 65.19 cannot arise from precision 46.42 and recall 49.61 under any averaging, and accuracy 49.61 duplicates the recall cell, down from 98.73 on a cleaner subset. We have not checked it against a rendered page, so a transcription artefact is not excluded; take the direction as evidence and the magnitude as unusable.

Two costs travel with it. Digitisation ran to about 1500 h for 85 of 138 sheets — the study itself uses 27 — against a cited 1250 h for 300,000 km on the Generalkarte. And the transfer our carried-versus-oracle analysis measures is here only asserted: the shared legend supposedly carries the method to other regions of the series, cross-source use being left to future transfer learning. They close by noting there is no common benchmark for the task, so lineage numbers cannot be lined up against ours without care.
