## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Shteriyanov and colleagues introduce BlueprintSymVL, a benchmark for vision-language model (VLM) symbol recognition on engineering blueprints, developed at McDermott, an energy-sector engineering contractor, under an Action Research design. It is, on our reading rather than the paper's claim, the closest published analogue to our task outside archaeology: a small, stereotyped symbol must be found and counted in a dense technical drawing, with the model shown what the symbol looks like at query time rather than trained on it. The data derive from a public synthetic Piping and Instrumentation Diagram corpus of 500 annotated blueprints, each split into 16 regions of 860x860 pixels. One hundred regions were curated across five symbol classes (gate, butterfly and check valves, reducer, spacer) and four scenarios (baseline, dense, similar distractors, target absent), five per combination, then duplicated with synthetic revision-cloud occlusions for 200 in total.

The evaluation design earns the source its place in our related-work section. Each query supplies a context-rich exemplar image with two instances of the target ringed in red, plus a query region; the model returns a count and the labels adjacent to each instance, scored by fuzzy string matching at an 85 per cent threshold. Three metrics are reported: Exact Match Rate (EMR — the share of regions where both the count and every label are correct), aggregate precision and recall over labels, and raw false-positive label counts on the negative regions. Four models ran at temperature 0, image detail high. Gemini 2.5 Pro led every aggregate (EMR 50.5 per cent, recall 74.48, precision 53.16), ahead of Qwen 2.5 VL 72B (40.5 / 71.35 / 43.49), GPT-4o (30 / 63.02 / 37.58) and InternVL 2.5 78B (4.5 / 26.69 / 15.32). Those aggregates pool the corpus's clean and occluded halves: our arithmetic on Table 6 recovers each Total Overall EMR as the mean of a model's regular and occluded scores, so Gemini's clean-region EMR is 63 and its occluded 38.

Three findings transfer. First, recall exceeds precision in every model — a structural bias towards over-detection, exactly the error our adversarial verifier pass is built to absorb, and one the negative-case tallies quantify (Qwen 38 false-positive labels, Gemini 48, GPT-4o 64, InternVL 91). Second, difficulty is not uniform: across the three strongest models mean EMR falls from 81.3 per cent on uncluttered baseline regions to 22.7 per cent where at least ten symbols share the region, and to 28.0 per cent where a visually similar distractor is present — and those regions hold only two or three symbols, so confusability degrades without clutter doing the work. Third, the exemplar's framing matters more than its content: a bare symbol crop in place of the context-rich example cost 14 points of EMR, while a different exemplar showing one ringed instance rather than two left EMR unchanged (precision still fell four points, from 53.16 to 48.96).

Caveats travel with all of it: 200 regions, five per class-scenario cell, one drawing genre, one prompt template, no repeated sampling or aggregation described, no confidence intervals.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

The symbol-on-technical-drawing analogue in its strongest available form: a generalist VLM asked to find and count a stereotyped symbol in a cluttered two-dimensional drawing from a single visual exemplar, with no fine-tuning — structurally our task with a scanned topographic sheet swapped for a P&ID, and with Gemini 2.5 Pro as its top performer, so its absolute numbers read as a same-family reference point rather than a cross-architecture guess — with the proviso that Exact Match Rate demands the exact count plus every adjacent text label at an 85 per cent fuzzy-match threshold and never asks for coordinates, so those figures index relative difficulty and failure-mode direction rather than a detection score commensurable with our F1 or MCC. Secondarily it supplies open-vocabulary / VLM-detection framing and a metric-hygiene lesson worth carrying: the authors' own diagnostic is the gap between aggregate precision and recall on one hand and per-region Exact Match Rate on the other, which is, on our reading and not the paper's, the counterpart of our tile-level versus object-level split — a parallel in aggregation unit rather than in severity, since EMR is the harsher instrument. Its sharpest contrast with us is architectural — temperature 0, one exemplar, and no aggregation or verifier described anywhere in the protocol — so on the consensus and verification axes the source marks the gap rather than filling it.

### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "When the exemplar symbol was cropped from its surrounding context, EMR dropped sharply to 36.5% and precision nearly halved (from 53.2% to 35.1%)"
- **Locator:** page_index 10 · p.11 · §5 Discussion — ablation on exemplar selection (Gemini 2.5 Pro only)
- **Paraphrase:** In an ablation run on the top model only, replacing the context-rich exemplar region with a tight decontextualised crop of the symbol dropped Gemini 2.5 Pro's Exact Match Rate from 50.5 to 36.5 per cent and precision from 53.16 to 35.07 per cent (Table 7; the discussion rounds these to 53.2 and 35.1). By contrast an alternate exemplar containing one ringed instance rather than two left EMR unchanged at 50.5 per cent, though recall and precision did move (74.48 to 73.44 and 53.16 to 48.96) — a shift the authors do not remark on when calling the result a validation of protocol robustness. The authors conclude that the surrounding visual context in the exemplar carries the signal, while the particular example chosen, and the number of instances it shows, do not.
