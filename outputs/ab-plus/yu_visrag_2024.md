## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Yu and colleagues build VisRAG, a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline in which document pages are never parsed into text: a vision-language model embeds the page image for retrieval (VisRAG-Ret), and a VLM reads the retrieved page images to answer the query (VisRAG-Gen). The comparison class throughout is TextRAG — the identical pipeline over OCR-extracted or model-captioned text. This is an information-retrieval paper published at ICLR 2025, not a detection paper: the evaluation is page-level visual question answering over six datasets (ArxivQA, ChartQA, MP-DocVQA, InfoVQA, PlotQA, SlideVQA), scored by MRR@10 and Recall@10 for retrieval and by relaxed exact-match accuracy, with a 5 per cent margin on numeric answers, for generation. It earns its place because it is the closest attested test of the proposition our own modality comparison puts to a map sheet: that handing a VLM the picture beats handing it a transcription of the picture.

Two comparisons come close to isolating the modality rather than the model. MiniCPM (Captioner) uses the same VLM, MiniCPM-V 2.0, to transcribe pages and then retrieves over that transcription with a 2.72 B MiniCPM text embedder; it loses to VisRAG-Ret, which encodes the page image with that same VLM (60.88 against 71.49 average MRR@10 out-of-domain, 69.78 against 77.91 in-domain) — the parser is held fixed, the retrieval encoder is not (3.43 B, adding the SigLIP vision encoder; Table 2). And in the Oracle condition, where the reader is given only the ground-truth page and no fine-tuning is used anywhere in generation, GPT-4o on page images averages 63.49 accuracy against 52.97 for GPT-4o on the parsed text of those same pages, ahead on all six datasets. That second result is the half that transfers to a prompt-only setting like ours; the retrieval half does not, VisRAG-Ret being fine-tuned on 240K synthetic pairs (plus in-domain VQA data for its in-domain numbers) across 8 A100 GPUs.

The aggregation findings speak to our consensus architecture. For a VLM that accepts one image, the authors compare concatenating retrieved pages into a single image against answering each page separately and selecting the highest-confidence answer. Answer-then-select degrades more gracefully — 35.50 against 28.96 at top-3 — but neither gains from extra pages, and the models that do gain are those pre-trained on multi-image input. Of those, only MiniCPM-V 2.6 was taken past top-3 — flat at top-6 (55.66 against 56.12), down at top-10 (49.30) — and the authors read that decline as open-source VLMs struggling with noisy retrieved pages rather than as a limit on visual evidence as such.

Read the numbers carefully; this is a relative-gains paper throughout. The abstract's 20–40 per cent end-to-end gain is 53.32 against 37.97 and 52.44 against 43.54 in Figure 1 — about fifteen and nine accuracy points — and the striking 25-to-51 per cent end-to-end jump is InfographicsVQA alone. The parenthesised percentages in the generation table are performance relative to Oracle, not accuracy — a second trap for a hurried reader, and incidentally the same carried-versus-oracle framing we use for transfer taxes.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Primary cluster: open-vocabulary / VLM detection alternative — specifically its retrieval-over-visual-documents corner, where the claim under test is not whether a VLM can localise an object but whether the page image is a better input than the page's transcription. For us it is the strongest external evidence behind the text arm of our modality comparison, and unusually clean, because one comparison is strictly model-fixed (GPT-4o on images versus GPT-4o on parsed text, both at Oracle) and a second holds the parsing VLM fixed (MiniCPM-V 2.0 as captioner versus as image encoder), though in that second pair the retrieval encoders differ in size and architecture (Table 2). It should be cited with three qualifications carried: its retrieval gains rest on fine-tuning we do not do, its tasks are page-level question answering rather than symbol localisation, and every headline improvement it reports is relative rather than in accuracy points — though one scope condition runs our way, its margin over text widening as the page carries less extractable text (§5.3).

## Key points (salience-ranked to the citing paper)

### KP1
- **Quote (verbatim):** "despite utilizing the same VLM MiniCPM-V 2.0 for parsing, MiniCPM (Captioner) performs worse than VisRAG-Ret, indicating that directly encoding with VLMs works better than using VLMs for parsing. This can be attributed to the inevitable information loss when multi-modality information is transcribed into text."
- **Locator:** page_index 7 · p.8 · §5.1 Overall Performance — Retrieval Performance
- **Paraphrase:** The same vision-language model, MiniCPM-V 2.0, is used two ways: to transcribe the page into text that is then encoded (MiniCPM (Captioner)), and to encode the page image directly (VisRAG-Ret). The direct-image route wins, which the authors read as information lost in transcription. The two retrievers are not identical, though: MiniCPM (Captioner) encodes with a 2.72 B MiniCPM text embedder, VisRAG-Ret with the 3.43 B MiniCPM-V 2.0 and its SigLIP vision encoder (Table 2), and Appendix I shows VisRAG-Ret beating MiniCPM (OCR) even on rendered screenshots of pure-text BEIR documents, where no visual information exists to be lost — so architecture as well as modality is in play. The margin is 60.88 against 71.49 average MRR@10 out-of-domain and 69.78 against 77.91 in-domain (Table 2). A further scope condition: both models are fine-tuned, so the finding is attested for trained retrievers, not for a prompted VLM.

### KP2
- **Quote (verbatim):** "For instance, MiniCPM-V 2.0 achieves 30% higher performance than MiniCPM (OCR) when using ground-truth documents. This underscores the importance of visual clues in extracting answers from documents."
- **Locator:** page_index 8 · p.9 · §5.1 Overall Performance — Generation Performance
- **Paraphrase:** Given only the ground-truth page (the 'Oracle' condition), a VLM reading the page image beats the language model it is built on reading OCR output by 30 per cent — relative, not in accuracy points: 41.58 against 31.92 average accuracy in Table 3. The same-model pair in the same table is cleaner still and points the same way: GPT-4o reading page images averages 63.49 at Oracle against 52.97 for GPT-4o reading the parsed text of those same pages, and is ahead on all six evaluation datasets. ### KP3
- **Quote (verbatim):** "the weighted selection approach demonstrates better performance than page concatenation when handling 2 or 3 retrieved documents. However, neither method shows a performance improvement as the number of retrieved documents increases"
- **Locator:** page_index 8 · p.9 · §5.1 Overall Performance — Generation Performance
- **Paraphrase:** Two ways of giving a single-image VLM more than one page are compared: concatenating the pages into one image, or answering each page separately and selecting the answer with the highest confidence, where confidence is the inverse perplexity of the answer weighted by the normalised retrieval score (§3.2.2). Answer-then-select is the more graceful — at top-3 it averages 35.50 against 28.96 for concatenation (Table 3) — but neither buys anything from extra pages. The same passage adds, hedged, that the models which do gain from more retrieved pages are those pre-trained on multiple images, 'suggesting that only VLMs pre-trained on multi-image data can effectively reason over multiple retrieved pages'.
- **Relevance:** §4 architecture — why we aggregate per-pass answers rather than enlarge the prompt · Consensus and aggregation over repeated or multiple inputs · **extends**

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "The cumulative improvements in both retrieval and generation phases result in an overall accuracy increment from 25% to 51%. Across the six evaluation datasets, VisRAG shows a 40% relative accuracy increment on average"
- **Locator:** page_index 8 · p.9 · §5.1 Overall Performance — End-to-end Performance
- **Paraphrase:** The 25-to-51 per cent jump is one dataset, InfographicsVQA (Figure 3), not the corpus; across the six datasets the headline is a 40 per cent relative increment, which in Figure 1's numbers is 53.32 against 37.97 — about fifteen accuracy points, not forty. The paper reports relative gains consistently and attributes the end-to-end figure to the cascade of a better retriever feeding a better reader rather than to modality alone. The retriever in that cascade is the fine-tuned VisRAG-Ret, so the end-to-end figure is not a prompt-only result either. Quoting '40%' as a points difference would overstate it by about two and a half times.
- **Relevance:** §2 related work — how to cite VisRAG's numbers without inflating them · Metric hygiene and reporting comparability · **complicates**

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "The generation part does not use any fine-tuning; we directly use off-the-shelf LLMs/VLMs for generation."
- **Locator:** page_index 6 · p.7 · §4 Experimental Methodology — Implementation Details
- **Paraphrase:** Every generation number in the paper comes from an off-the-shelf model with no task-specific training. The training sits entirely upstream: VisRAG-Ret and the baseline retrievers for one epoch at batch size 128 on 8 NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs, plus the MiniCPM-V 2.0 captioner used for the (Captioner) parsing condition (Appendix B.2). The reading half of the pipeline is therefore a zero-training, prompt-only result of the kind our own design produces, whereas the retrieval half is not, and its gains should not be carried into a claim about prompted VLMs.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — what transfers to a zero-training, prompt-only pipeline · Cost, training budget, and who can run the method · **supports**

### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "the term "Club Jetty" in the relevant document is not successfully extracted due to its decorative font. This leads to TextRAG failing to retrieve the document, while VisRAG successfully retrieves it."
- **Locator:** page_index 20 · p.21 · Appendix F Case Study — DocVQA case (Table 8)
- **Paraphrase:** A worked failure case: an ornamental typeface defeats the OCR stage — the parsing result printed in Table 8 reads CLUBIETTY — so the searchable term never enters the text index, and the text pipeline cannot retrieve the page at all, while the image pipeline can. This is one illustrative case, not a measured error class, but the mechanism is the one that matters for scanned historical maps, where lettering is engraved, rotated, curved, or interleaved with line work and a parse-first pipeline silently drops what it cannot read. The companion case on the same page shows the other half of the mechanism — layout loss leaving it unclear which of two percentages belongs to which region.
- **Relevance:** §2 related work — the failure mode that motivates reading the map image directly · Historical-map extraction — why parse-first pipelines fail on non-standard glyphs · **supports**

### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "The results indicate significant performance degradation when handling a larger number of retrieved pages, for both page concatenation (MiniCPM-V 2.0) and multi-page input (MiniCPM-V 2.6)."
- **Locator:** page_index 23 · p.24 · Appendix G Additional Retrieval and Generation Results
- **Paraphrase:** Pushing to six and ten retrieved pages degrades both strategies: page concatenation falls from 36.16 at top-1 to 25.41 at top-6 and 23.60 at top-10, and the multi-image MiniCPM-V 2.6 from 56.12 at top-3 to 55.66 at top-6 and 49.30 at top-10 (Tables 3 and 11). The multi-image model is the more robust of the two, and the authors attribute the decline to open-source VLMs struggling to extract relevant information from noisy retrieved pages, so context size and retrieval precision are confounded here; Qwen2-VL, the strongest model in Table 11, was tested only to top-3 and was still improving there. Attested for two open-source VLMs rather than for VLMs generally, it is still a caution for any design tempted to enlarge the image or stack more tiles rather than aggregate over separate reads.
- **Relevance:** §4 architecture — tiling and prompt-size decisions · Context ceilings — how much visual evidence to feed at once · **complicates**

- **Evaluation denominators after filtering** — Table 1 reports, per source dataset, the evaluation query count and the share of the original queries preserved after context-dependent questions were filtered out: ArXivQA 816 (8 per cent), ChartQA 63 (5 per cent), MP-DocVQA 591 (11 per cent), InfoVQA 718 (26 per cent), PlotQA 863 (4 per cent), and SlideVQA 556 (25 per cent). Between 74 and 96 per cent of each source dataset's queries were therefore discarded, and every per-dataset accuracy elsewhere in the paper rests on these denominators — ChartQA on 63 questions, where a single item is worth roughly 1.6 accuracy points (our arithmetic on the printed count). (page_index 5 · p.6 · §3.3 Data Construction — Table 1, Dataset statistics)
- **An LLM built the evaluation set** — The filter that removed context-dependent queries from the evaluation sets was itself a large language model: GPT-4o, prompted with human-annotated samples drawn from DocVQA. The authors concede that a small number of such queries may survive filtering, but assert without measurement that their presence is minimal and does not significantly affect the quality of the dataset. (page_index 16 · p.17 · Appendix A.2 Query Filtering)
- **Training-data efficiency** — VisRAG-Ret matches the retrieval performance of the bge-large (OCR) text embedder after training on only 20K examples, where the MiniCPM (OCR) text baseline needs about 75K, and it retains a 13 per cent advantage over MiniCPM (OCR) later in training. The 13 per cent is a relative gain, as throughout this paper, and all three are fine-tuned retrievers rather than prompted models. (page_index 8 · p.9 · §5.2 Training Data Efficiency — Figure 4)
- **Rendered text beats raw text** — On three text-only subsets of the BEIR retrieval benchmark, VisRAG-Ret working from rendered screenshots of the documents outscores MiniCPM (OCR) working from the raw text: NDCG@10 of 62.47 against 61.04 on SciFact, 27.02 against 14.12 on NFCorpus, and 16.25 against 13.01 on Scidocs. Rendering plain text as an image and encoding it visually thus beat encoding the same text directly, on documents that hold no visual information to be lost in transcription. (page_index 24 · p.25 · Appendix I Retrieval Performance on Text Retrieval Benchmarks — Table 13)
- **Pooling, not modality** — The authors credit that text-only result to architecture rather than modality: VisRAG-Ret's pooling-based representation, they argue, captures textual detail well and suits text-heavy retrieval. Because the two systems are separately fine-tuned models rather than one model given two kinds of input, the contrast does not isolate modality — a qualification that carries across to the paper's MiniCPM (Captioner) comparison, where the retrieval encoders also differ. (page_index 24 · p.25 · Appendix I Retrieval Performance on Text Retrieval Benchmarks)
- **The 58 per cent saving does not reconcile** — Table 12 gives offline latency per document as 284 ms parsing plus 28 ms encoding (312 ms in total) for MiniCPM (OCR) against 121 ms encoding and no parsing for VisRAG-Ret, and the prose reports this as a 58 per cent reduction in total document processing time; 121 ms against 312 ms is a 61 per cent reduction, so the stated figure does not follow from the table beside it. Online per-query latency is identical for the two (28 ms encoding plus 26 ms search), the queries being text either way, so the saving is wholly an indexing-time saving. (page_index 24 · p.25 · Appendix H Retrieval Efficiency — Table 12)
- **Late fusion trails one model** — Fusing the rankings of the text and vision baselines with reciprocal rank fusion gives a meaningful gain over either modality alone, but still falls short of VisRAG-Ret, which averages 71.49 MRR@10 out-of-domain and 77.91 in-domain. Combining a text retriever with an image retriever after the fact is therefore worse than a single model that encodes the page image. (page_index 23 · p.24 · Appendix G Additional Retrieval and Generation Results — Table 10)
- **ColPali's cost is memory** — The paper's stronger claim against ColPali, the competing page-image retriever, is storage rather than accuracy: ColPali represents a page as 1030 vectors of 128 dimensions, about 256 KB, where VisRAG-Ret uses a single 2304-dimensional vector of about 4.5 KB. The ColPali figure is attributed to Faysse and colleagues rather than measured here. (page_index 7 · p.8 · §5.1 Overall Performance — Retrieval Performance)
- **ColPali's accuracy gap is small** — In the in-domain retrieval table ColPali averages 76.54 MRR@10 against VisRAG-Ret's 77.91 — a gap of 1.37 points — and leads VisRAG-Ret on two of the six evaluation datasets, DocVQA (82.79 against 75.37) and SlideVQA (93.99 against 91.85). VisRAG-Ret's margin over the nearest page-image competitor is therefore slight, which is why the paper's case against ColPali rests on memory rather than on accuracy. (page_index 6 · p.7 · §4 Experimental Methodology — Table 2(c), in-domain MRR@10)
- **Same gap on Recall@10** — The in-domain ordering holds on Recall@10 with a comparable margin: ColPali averages 89.72 against VisRAG-Ret's 92.20 across the six evaluation datasets, leading on DocVQA (94.75 against 91.20) but on none of the other five. (page_index 20 · p.21 · Appendix D Retrieval Performance in Recall@10 — Table 6)
- **ColPali was retrained here** — The ColPali rows are not ColPali as released: the authors trained it on their own dataset, using the official code and the hyper-parameters published with it. That makes the comparison one between two models trained on the same data, which is fairer than an off-the-shelf contrast but is not a reproduction of ColPali's own reported numbers. (page_index 6 · p.7 · §4 Experimental Methodology — Retrieval Experiments, in-domain setting)
