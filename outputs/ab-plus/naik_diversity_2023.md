## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Naik and colleagues make a claim that lands on our aggregation design: self-consistency-style ensembling draws its diversity from the decoder, and decoder diversity does not reliably deliver diversity of solution approach. Their formulation — stochasticity-based methods guide diversity "at the token level" rather than at the level of thought or method, so that "linguistic token diversity does not always ensure diverse and independent solution approaches" — is the sharpest available statement of the assumption our consensus-over-passes architecture inherits, since we resample one fixed proposer prompt and let sampling supply the variation. Their alternative, DIVERSEPROMPTING, has the model itself name reasoning approaches and personas for a task, rewrites Wei et al.'s few-shot examples into each approach, then either runs each prompt in its own call (DIV-SE) or packs all approaches into one call (IDIV-SE), aggregating by majority vote.

The evidence is text reasoning benchmarks on GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4, and LLaMA-2 70B — no vision, no localisation, no detection — so what transfers is the design argument and the cost framing, not the numbers. Self-consistency scales weakly where the task is hard: in Table 1 (GPT-4, zero-shot CoT) BLOCKSWORLD 4/5 moves only from 40.00 at CoT to 41.20 at SC-10, with SC-3 (38.20) and SC-5 (38.40) below it, while GRAPH COLORING climbs from 15.0 at CoT to 23.0 at SC-10. The rows differ in more than pass count: the SC runs sample at T = 0.7 while every other approach is greedy at T = 0, so the sub-baseline dips mix temperature with pass count, and DIV-SE and IDIV-SE carry no decoder diversity at all. More passes of one prompt can still buy little — a caution for our K=3 versus K=10 ladder. The paper also plots accuracy against estimated dollar cost and claims Pareto dominance, the trade-space we report; concretely, their prompt-diverse approach in the zero-shot setting beats SC-10 in the few-shot setting at 4× lower cost on BLOCKSWORLD 3 with GPT-4 — a comparison that crosses prompting settings rather than holding them fixed.

Three caveats travel with the headlines. The GRAPH COLORING figure (DIV-SE 97.00 against a 15.0 CoT baseline) is not a majority vote: valid colourings are not comparable by exact string match, so an external sound verifier picks the correct solution out of the ensemble. That is both an antecedent for our verifier pass and a warning that verifier-assisted selection is a different operating point from consensus. Second, the approach-and-persona configuration is chosen once, on a validation set the paper's footnote sizes at fewer than 20 items, using GPT-3.5 Turbo, then reused across every model evaluated and across tasks for MATH and GRAPH COLORING — their version of our carried-versus-oracle transfer, with no transfer tax reported. Third, Table 4 shows one well-chosen persona lifting AQUA-RAT on GPT-3.5 by 8.27 p.p with no ensembling at all, so an unknown share of the ensemble gain is single-prompt quality. A fourth is ours, not the paper's: several headline percentage-point figures do not reconcile with its own tables (overflow notes).

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

A consensus / test-time-compute antecedent that argues against the diversity source our architecture uses: Naik et al. treat decoder stochasticity as the weak form of diversity and prompt-level variation in reasoning approach as the strong form, and report self-consistency scaling almost flat on their hardest planning benchmark (BLOCKSWORLD 4/5, GPT-4 zero-shot: 40.00 at single-pass CoT, 41.20 at SC-10) where prompt-diverse ensembles move much further. It is a text-reasoning paper, so it constrains our claims rather than supplying comparable numbers: it says our repeated passes vary at the token level, that token variety does not always ensure independent solution approaches, and that a cheap prompt-level manipulation — alternative symbol-reading strategies, or an explicit persona — is an untested and possibly stronger diversity axis than K. Secondarily it is a methods/metrics source, both for the accuracy-versus-dollar-cost frontier it plots and for two hygiene traps it illustrates: its state-of-the-art GRAPH COLORING number is selected by an external sound verifier rather than by vote, and its prompt configuration is chosen on fewer than 20 validation items and then reused across models and tasks.

- **Decoding conditions and ensemble sizes** — The decoding conditions are asymmetric by design: the self-consistency baselines sample at temperature T = 0.7 with no top-k truncation and draw up to ten outputs (SC-s, s in [1, 10]), while every other approach — the chain-of-thought baseline and the prompt-diverse ensembles alike — runs greedily at T = 0. Ensemble size also varies by benchmark: five members for GSM8K and AQUA-RAT, three for the planning, GRAPH COLORING, and COMMONSENSEQA benchmarks. On those three benchmarks the prompt-diverse rows are therefore three-member ensembles set against baselines drawing up to ten samples, and they carry no decoder-borne diversity at all. (page_index 4 · p.5 · §3 Experiments — Baselines)
- **Error propagation in one call** — Error propagation in the single-call variant (IDIV-SE) is estimated on AQUA-RAT in the few-shot-CoT setting only, by re-attempting the last two approaches in a separate session and counting a case as propagation when the model does not reproduce its original answer within three attempts. On that measure GPT-4 propagates at 6.2% and GPT-3.5 at 5.5% — rates the authors themselves describe as comparable to the performance difference between DIV-SE (separate calls) and IDIV-SE (one call), and treat as a leading explanation for that difference. (page_index 6 · p.7 · §4.3 Errors & Prompt Utility — Error Propagation)
- **Instruction-following capability floor** — Prompt-diverse ensembling bought nothing for the weaker model on the planning benchmark: with GPT-3.5 the baselines are 6% on BLOCKSWORLD 3 and 0.6% on BLOCKSWORLD 4/5, and neither IDIV-SE nor DIV-SE improved on them. The authors attribute this to GPT-3.5 too often failing to follow the restrictions stated in the problem instructions, producing infeasible or incorrect plans, and conclude that instruction-following capability is crucial to the methods they propose. (page_index 6 · p.7 · §4.1.3 Planning via BLOCKSWORLD — GPT-3.5 Results)
- **Ensembling backfires on weaker model** — On AQUA-RAT with an 8-bit quantised LLaMA-2 70B, both prompt-diverse methods gain over 10 p.p in the few-shot-CoT setting (CoT 29.1 against IDIV-SE 39.7 and DIV-SE 39.9) but the zero-shot gain is negligible: IDIV-SE scores 27.00 against a CoT baseline of 31.32, and DIV-SE only 32.00. The authors hypothesise that the model cannot follow both the instructions and the nominated approach without examples — so the method is not model-agnostic, and on a weak instruction-follower one variant fell below the single-pass baseline. (page_index 6 · p.7 · §4.2 Open Source Models — Table 3)
- **Saturation ceiling on GSM8K** — GSM8K shows the saturation boundary within a single benchmark. With GPT-4 the zero-shot and few-shot CoT baselines already stand at 94% and 95%, IDIV-SE produces no significant gain in either setting, and DIV-SE reaches 96.3%; with GPT-3.5 the same methods add 5.31 p.p (IDIV-SE) and 10.39 p.p (DIV-SE) over a 76.11% zero-shot baseline, and 7.68 p.p (DIV-SE) over an 81.4% few-shot baseline. (page_index 10 · p.11 · Appendix C.2 — Arithmetic reasoning via GSM8K)

The authors give two reasons: the task does not demand as much reasoning and thought diversity as their other benchmarks, and the dataset contains ambiguous questions that read verbatim may admit many plausible answers while the ground truth records only one.
