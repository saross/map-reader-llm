## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Zhang and colleagues propose Consensus Entropy (CE), a training-free, label-free reliability score for optical character recognition (OCR) computed from the disagreement among several vision-language models' outputs on the same image: correct predictions converge in output space while errors diverge, so the entropy of pairwise output similarities stands in for a confidence estimate. CE then drives a two-stage pipeline, CE-OCR: samples below a threshold gate are merged by an entropy-weighted token-level ensemble, samples above it are routed to a stronger model that rephrases the image together with the candidate outputs. Evaluation covers OCRBench (scored 0-1000), OCRBench-V2, CCOCR, and a curated 1,000-page human-scored PDF set.

This is the closest published antecedent to our consensus-over-passes plus adversarial-verifier architecture, and it is most useful where it complicates our design rather than endorses it. The endorsement is real but narrower than it looks: with one model sampled three times at temperature 0.7, CE-Ensemble beat every individual run (Qwen2VL-7B: runs of 834, 827 and 832, ensemble 852), so agreement carries signal without architectural diversity — the regime we work in. The baseline there is the three temperature-0.7 runs, not the model's greedy pass, and against the greedy scores printed elsewhere in the paper two of the three identical-model ensembles lose (875 against 888, 870 against 879). The complication is the base rate. Sweeping all 55,154 three- to five-model combinations drawn from 24 models on OCRBench, the ensemble beat the best participating model in 66.2 per cent of triples, rising to 91.11 per cent at five models; the paper's 'always avoids the worst predictions' is the 100 per cent column, which is measured against the weakest member. At K=3 — our own configuration — about a third of three-model combinations gained nothing over the best single pass already in the group, had anything identified it (the unit is a combination's aggregate benchmark score, not an individual output).

Two results bear on cost. Self-consistency at three samples underperformed — 828 on average, 2.8 per cent below the single-model average of 852, its best configuration (875) below that same model's greedy single pass (888) — though self-consistency runs at temperature 0.7 against greedy singles at 0.0, so aggregation rule and decoding temperature are confounded. And the ablation prices the routed stage: the ensemble alone, with nothing routed, reaches 902 — 99.4 per cent of full performance — while routing 7.3 per cent of inputs to a stronger model adds the last five points. Consensus carries the gain; the routed second pass is the expensive increment.

Metric hygiene needs watching. The abstract's 42.1 per cent verification gain is the largest of three (20.0, 42.1, 28.1), and the 'average improvement of 15.2 in F1' is the largest of the three absolute differences rather than their mean. Band-wise, CE loses to VLM-as-Judge in the top human-score band for two of three reference models; its advantage lives in the lower bands. The limitations concede that the routing threshold needs empirical re-calibration per domain — our transfer tax under another name.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Consensus / test-time-compute antecedent, and the nearest one in the corpus to our architecture: an agreement signal over repeated model outputs used both to verify predictions and to decide when to spend more compute, gated by a threshold that is our carried-versus-oracle operating point in miniature. It supports the design where it shows that agreement among repeated samples of a single model still improves selection among those samples without architectural diversity, and complicates it where it prices the win: at three participants the ensemble beats its best member only about two-thirds of the time, both self-consistency and, for two of three models, same-model CE ensembles underperformed a greedy single pass, and the routed stronger-model stage buys roughly half a per cent of relative performance for a second pass on 7.3 per cent of inputs. Secondarily it is a metric-hygiene exhibit and a calibration-transfer one — its headline verification gains are best-of-three rather than averages, and its own limitations name per-domain threshold re-calibration as the barrier to out-of-the-box deployment.

- **Locator:** page_index 0 · p.1
- **Why:** The whole architecture in eleven words, and the assumption our consensus-over-passes design also rests on: that error is unsystematic enough to scatter, and correctness a narrow enough target to be hit repeatedly. - **Classical voting collapses** — Under a matched three-model setup across four task families, classical word-level majority voting (ROVER, imported from speech recognition) scores 57.5 / 50.7 / 3.2 / 50.4 on OCR, Doc-VQA, Math and Knowledge-Reasoning against a best-single-model baseline of 61.2 / 87.5 / 40.0 / 60.3 — an average change of −33.8 per cent, far worse than not aggregating at all. (page_index 7 · p.8 · §4.6 Comparison with Classical Baselines — Table 9)
- **Agreement geometry decides** — The authors trace both failures to a mismatch between the representation in which agreement is measured and the geometry of the outputs being aggregated: discrete word-level voting collapses (Doc-VQA −42.1 per cent, Math-VQA −92.0 per cent) because vision-language model outputs are diverse and non-aligned in phrasing, while semantic clustering over embeddings degrades on Doc-VQA (−10.1 per cent) and Knowledge-Reasoning (−3.5 per cent) because the character-level differences that matter for OCR are invisible to those embeddings. A consensus rule tuned for one output geometry can therefore be worse than no aggregation at all. (page_index 7 · p.8 · §4.6 Comparison with Classical Baselines — discussion)
- **Verbosity inflates the signal** — Output formatting contaminates the agreement signal: verbosity artificially inflates a model's consensus-entropy values, and cleaning the outputs with regular expressions and an LLM-based filter restores the expected monotonic relationship between threshold and accuracy. For GPT-4o the cleaned accuracy exceeds the original at every threshold reported — 0.930 against 0.844 at CE ≤ 0.1, and 0.928 against 0.833 at CE ≤ 0.2 — so an agreement statistic computed over un-normalised outputs is partly measuring format rather than content. (page_index 16 · p.17 · §B.5 GPT-4o Output Verbosity Analysis — Table 16)
- **Ranking beats calibration** — On Qwen2.5VL-72B predictions, consensus entropy separates reliable from unreliable outputs better than a VLM-as-Judge prompt on all three metrics reported, and the gap is widest on area under the curve: 0.9226 against the judge's 0.7727 with diverse reference models, and 0.9181 against 0.8041 with related ones. (page_index 15 · p.16 · §B.3 Model Calibration Analysis — Table 14)
