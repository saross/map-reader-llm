## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Ren's single-authored paper in the International Journal of Advanced Computer Science and Applications (IJACSA) builds a degradation-aware restoration workflow for scanned historical maps and — the part that matters to us — measures what restoration buys downstream. The corpus is 120 sheets (84 training, 18 validation, 18 test) at 300–600 dpi, tiled to 512×512 pixels with 25 per cent overlap, spanning urban-block, transport, water-system, administrative and topographic-symbol maps dated between 1890 and 1985. Each block carries a scalar degradation intensity built from colour attenuation, noise proportion, edge continuity and text adhesion, banded as mild (below 0.35), moderate (0.35–0.65) and severe (above 0.65), and used to balance samples and stratify evaluation, explicitly not as a separate optimisation objective. Six enhancement methods (histogram equalisation, CLAHE, Retinex, single-scale sharpening, low-light enhancement, and the author's multiscale model G6) are evaluated against the unenhanced original G0 on the same 18 test maps as paired statistical units; 95 per cent confidence intervals come from 2,000 bootstrap resamples and are reported for G6; significance is a two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test of G6 against the strongest baseline for each metric, Holm-corrected across the five quality metrics.

The results we can use are the downstream ones, all obtained with downstream parameters held fixed. Registration root-mean-square error falls from 8.31±1.42 px to 4.12±0.86 px; line-vectorisation F1 rises from 0.713±0.052 to 0.871±0.031; place-name optical character recognition (OCR) accuracy from 62.4±6.8 per cent to 84.7±4.1 per cent; six-class semantic-segmentation mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) from 0.614±0.047 to 0.802±0.029. Map-symbol recognition, the closest analogue to our task, averages 58.9 per cent before enhancement and 82.3 per cent after, and is weakest on stained sheets (55.8 to 77.6 per cent). External validation freezes the complete G6 configuration before touching the 144-sample public Semap test partition: mIoU 74.2±6.8 to 79.6±5.9 per cent, a gain of 5.4 percentage points at Holm-adjusted p<0.001.

Three intersections. First, this is direct evidence that image condition, not only model capability, sets the ceiling on symbol legibility in this lineage; we feed raw Soviet-era scans to a vision-language model with no restoration stage, and Ren is the strongest evidence we have that adding one is an untested lever. Second, freeze-the-configuration-then-transfer is our carried-operating-point discipline in another idiom, and Ren reports the transfer gain rather than assuming it. Third, the symbol extractor is template matching with a similarity threshold of 0.70 — a closed vocabulary — which is precisely the constraint an open-vocabulary prompt removes.

Read the numbers carefully. ‘Recognition rate’ is a bare rate with no stated denominator and no precision/recall split, even though the same table names false positives as a leading error type; and the headline comprehensive quality score of 0.872 comes from Eq. (4), whose minimum and maximum are by the paper's own definition taken ‘across all methods’, so it ranks G6 within this baseline set rather than measuring quality on any absolute scale. Reference [9] is Sobotkova, Ross and colleagues (2023), so the lineage Ren draws on already contains our own team's novice-volunteer work.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Historical-map extraction lineage, and an unusual entry in it for measuring point-symbol recognition as a function of image condition rather than of model architecture: symbol recognition on Ren's own sheets moves from 58.9 per cent to 82.3 per cent purely by restoring the scan, with no change of detector. It sits upstream of our pipeline rather than alongside it — there is no detector here to benchmark against ours, and its symbol extractor is closed-vocabulary template matching at a 0.70 similarity threshold — so its use to us is as a preprocessing-lever argument and as a second instance of freeze-then-transfer discipline (the complete configuration frozen before an unseen 144-sample public test partition, +5.4 pp mIoU) to set beside our carried operating points. Secondarily it is a methods/metrics cautionary case: bare recognition rates without denominators, and a composite quality score min–max normalised across the very baselines it is used to beat.

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "Candidate objects with a score above 0.65 go to layer coding, and those below the threshold are kept as a sample for manual review, thus reducing the number of false detections due to spots, folds, and paper grains."
