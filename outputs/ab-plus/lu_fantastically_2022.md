## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Lu, Bartolo, Moore, Riedel and Stenetorp (ACL 2022, pp. 8086-8098) show that the *order* of the few-shot examples inside an in-context-learning prompt — same examples, same template, only the permutation changed — can swing text-classification accuracy between near state-of-the-art and random guessing. The scope bounds everything below: eleven text-classification datasets, four GPT-2 sizes (0.1B-1.5B) and GPT-3 at 2.7B and 175B, 4-shot except 1-shot DBPedia and 2-shot AGNews, 24 permutations across 5 randomly drawn sample sets for 120 runs per experiment, evaluated on 256-example subsamples of each validation set. The order effect is established across tasks, model sizes, templates, sample sets and shot counts. Adding examples lifts the mean but not the stability; a permutation worth 88.7% on GPT2-XL (1.5B) falls to 51.6% on GPT2-Large (0.8B); pairwise Spearman correlations between models' permutation rankings hover around zero (0.05 between the 2.7B and 175B models). The authors' comparison point is that different fine-tuning initialisations typically move test performance by under 1% standard deviation.

The annotation-budget argument speaks most directly to us. If some orders are good and you cannot tell which without labels, the obvious fix — hold out a development set and pick the winner — spends annotation the few-shot setting is supposed not to have. Their answer is to sample an unlabelled 'probing set' from the model itself under every candidate permutation and rank permutations by the entropy of their predicted-label distributions (GlobalE, LocalE), keeping the top k = 4 of 24. GlobalE buys an average 13% relative improvement over the all-permutations baseline and LocalE 9.6%, up to 30% for models that suffer from high prompt variance, with lower variance too. Alongside it they report an oracle row that picks the top four orderings on validation-set performance: we read that oracle-versus-selected structure as the text-domain analogue of our carried-versus-oracle reporting; the analogy is ours, not theirs.

Three negative results are the ones we should carry. Calibrating the output distribution after Zhao et al. (2021) raised accuracy but left the variance high — shown on a single panel, 2-shot SST-2 on GPT2-XL — which cautions against reading a calibration step as a stability fix. Prompt selection only helps where the model already has some competence at the task — on RTE and CB the GPT-2 models sat at chance and no ordering rescued them — which bounds what prompt engineering can buy on the hard rungs of a difficulty ladder. And splitting the tiny training set into train and dev did beat the baseline, but underperformed entropy probing at every GPT-2 size tested — a single-dataset comparison, SST-2 only (Table 5) — so on that evidence the labelled-holdout route is not merely expensive but also worse.

Caveats that must travel: this is 2022, text classification, GPT-2 and GPT-3, no vision; scale reduces the effect and the 175B row is flatter than the GPT-2 rows on most tasks; that row rests on 2 sample sets of 12 permutations rather than 5 of 24; and the '13%' is relative improvement, not absolute accuracy.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Primary cluster: few-shot / in-context learning / annotation budget — this is the paper that first studied sample ordering in in-context learning in detail, by its own claim ('to the best of our knowledge, none has studied order sensitivity in detail'), and it establishes that permutation of in-context exemplars is a first-class design variable rather than a formatting detail, and that selecting among prompt variants with a labelled development set silently leaves the few-shot regime it claims to be measuring. For us it does two jobs: it is the citation for treating any ordered content in a prompt as a variable that needs either control or a label-free selection rule, and its oracle-versus-selected reporting is a text-domain antecedent of our carried-versus-oracle operating points and transfer taxes. It also touches the consensus / test-time-compute cluster — the probing set is a label-free selection signal built from the model's own generations — and the calibration cluster, since post-hoc calibration lifted their mean without shrinking the spread; the transfer to our setting is by analogy only, the evidence being text classification with 2022-era GPT models rather than vision-language detection.

### KP2
- **Quote (verbatim):** "In a fully-supervised setting, we could rely on a development set to select among sample orders. However, this is not desirable in a few-shot setting where the size of the development set is very limited, even unavailable"

- **Paraphrase:** Choosing among prompt variants by scoring them on held-out labelled data is available in the fully-supervised setting but not in a genuine few-shot one, where the development set is tiny or does not exist. Selection is therefore an annotation cost, not a free post-processing step.

### KP3
- **Quote (verbatim):** "We also provide an oracle to show the upper-bound of performance by selecting the top four performant orderings based on prompt performance on the validation set."

- **Paraphrase:** The paper reports an explicit oracle condition in which the four best orderings are chosen using validation-set performance, presented as an upper bound rather than an attainable operating point. It is a ceiling because selection spends labelled validation performance, which the true few-shot setting does not have. §4.1 adds that all evaluation is on 256-example subsamples of the validation sets, so selection and scoring may well draw on the same examples — the paper does not say either way, and that reading is ours.

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "We find that a specific permutation's performance may drop from 88.7% to 51.6% by changing the underlying model from GPT2-XL (1.5B) to GPT2-Large (0.8B)."

- **Paraphrase:** A single ordering scoring 88.7% on GPT2-XL collapses to 51.6% when the same prompt is run on GPT2-Large; the paper backs this with near-zero pairwise Spearman correlations between models' permutation rankings, including 0.05 between the 2.7B and 175B models. A prompt configuration tuned on one model carries no guarantee to another, even within the same family.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — why a configuration chosen on one model is re-selected, not assumed, on another · Few-shot / in-context learning — non-transferability of prompt configurations across models · **complicates**

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "increasing the number of training samples leads to increases in performance. However, a high level of variance remains, even with a large number of samples and can even increase. Based on this, we draw the conclusion that order sensitivity is likely to be a fundamental issue of In-context Learning regardless of the number of training samples."
- **Locator:** page_index 2 · p.3 · §2 Order Sensitivity and Prompt Design — adding training samples does not significantly reduce variance
- **Paraphrase:** Going from 1 to 32 shots on the GPT-2 family raises mean accuracy but does not shrink the spread across orderings, and can widen it, so the authors treat order sensitivity as intrinsic to in-context learning rather than a small-sample artefact. Buying more exemplars is not a way to buy stability.
- **Relevance:** §5 discussion — marginal return on annotation spend · Annotation budget — what additional exemplars do and do not purchase · **extends**

### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "Instead, we use the generative nature of language models to construct an unlabelled artificial development set and refer to it as a probing set. As the probing set is unlabelled, we use the predicted label distribution statistics and propose entropy-based metrics to measure the quality of candidate prompts."
- **Locator:** page_index 1 · p.2 · §1 Introduction — contribution statement
- **Paraphrase:** In place of labelled held-out data the authors sample text from the model itself under each candidate prompt to build an unlabelled probing set, then rank prompts by entropy statistics of the label distribution the model predicts over it. The selection signal is the model's own behaviour, costing inference rather than annotation.
- **Relevance:** §4 architecture — selecting an operating point without spending gold-standard labels · Consensus / test-time compute — label-free selection signals built from a model's own outputs · **extends**

### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "we find that although calibration leads to much higher performance, the variance remains high"

## Optional framing hook (not counted in the salience cap)

- **Quote (verbatim):** "This order sensitivity is universal across models, and although increasing the model size somewhat addresses it, the problem is still present for some text classification tasks (Subj in Figure 1) for models with billions of parameters."

- **Why:** The one line that states the phenomenon and its own limit in the same breath, which is why it is the honest hook rather than the 'fantastic permutations' flourish: order sensitivity is universal, scale helps, scale does not cure.
