## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Sterzinger, Peer, and Sablatnig adapt frozen vision foundation models to historical-map segmentation with as little machinery as the problem allows: extract patch embeddings from DINOv2, SAM, or RADIO, upsample them to the input size, fit a single linear classifier pixel-wise, and add low-rank adaptation (DoRA, rank 4 when k ≤ 10). On the Siegfried benchmark of Swiss topographic sheets they lead every few-shot column against MapSAM and against U-Net, DeepLabV3+, and SegFormer baselines they trained themselves; on the ICDAR 2021 building-block task they reach a mean panoptic quality of 67.3 without tuning for that shape-aware metric, which would have placed them between the competition's first place (74.1) and its actual second (62.6). The adaptation costs 689k trainable parameters, 0.21 per cent of the model.

Three things bear on us. First, the annotation-budget curve. The railway supervision axis runs from 5,872 labelled 224 × 224 tiles down to a single tile, and Table 3 walks it: F1 96.7 at full data, 94.9 at 58 tiles, 89.4 at ten, 86.1 at five, 52.4 at one. Ten labelled tiles retain roughly 92 per cent of the full-data F1 (our ratio); one tile retains just over half. That is the trained-adaptation competitor to our zero-training prompted approach, priced in labels rather than tokens.

Second, the difficulty ladder. Siegfried's two halves are railways, standing for linear features, and vineyards, standing for areal features; the third task is closed building blocks on Paris maps. Every target is an extended area or line. No point symbol is evaluated anywhere, and no task requires telling one symbol family from dozens of others. This is, on our reading, the lineage's strongest recent result — and it sits on the rung below ours.

Third, adaptation closes most of the headroom. With the encoder frozen and only the linear probe trained on ten samples, RADIO reaches IoU 52.4 on railways; DoRA lifts that to 83.7. The authors' diagnosis is a domain gap stated about symbols specifically — SAM's feature space "might not align well with the semantic meaning of cartographic symbols" — compounded by within-map confusability, since roads, railways, and river patterns look alike while meaning different things. Neither claim is measured directly, and both are hypotheses about our substrate as much as theirs.

Two metric-hygiene notes. The headline vineyard gains reproduce against MapSAM, not the strongest baseline the authors trained themselves: MapSAM → Ours is +13.2 and +22.7 per cent where SegFormer → Ours is +6.9 and +7.8. Railway 5-shot behaves the same way (+19.8 against MapSAM, +11.1 against SegFormer); railway 10-shot does not, MapSAM (78.5 IoU) leading the baselines there. And at full data on railways their F1 leads the table (96.7) while their IoU is fourth (92.0, behind SegFormer 93.0, DeepLabV3+ 92.9, and U-Net 92.1); the paper scopes its outperformance claim to few-shot settings correctly, but which metric one headlines changes the ranking. Their own numbers are means or medians over at least three runs, ten where k ≤ 5; no table or passage reports a dispersion statistic.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Historical-map extraction lineage, and — our judgement, not the paper's — the strongest few-shot result currently in it: a frozen RADIO encoder with low-rank adaptation and a linear pixel head that leads MapSAM and self-trained CNN and transformer baselines down to ten, five, and a single labelled tile on both Siegfried sub-datasets. It sits one rung below us on the difficulty ladder — every target is areal or linear (vineyards, railways, building blocks) rather than a point symbol competing with dozens of other symbol families — and it prices the competing route in a currency we do not spend: 689k trainable parameters and five to ten labelled 224 × 224 tiles, against our zero training and per-token inference. Its second use to us is as a metric-hygiene exhibit, since the abstract's relative gains reproduce against MapSAM but shrink to roughly seven to eight per cent against the authors' own SegFormer baseline in the vineyard few-shot cells, and at full data their F1 leads where their IoU does not.

## Optional framing hook (not counted in the salience cap)

- **Quote (verbatim):** "Our approach exemplifies Occam's Razor: when a simpler method outperforms more complex ones, added complexity becomes not only unnecessary but even detrimental, as more parameters increase the risk of overfitting and reduce generalization."
- **Locator:** page_index 2 · p.3
- **Why:** A clean statement of the parsimony argument from inside the historical-map extraction lineage, and one that cuts both ways for us. But the same razor is the obvious objection to consensus-over-passes plus an adversarial verifier, which is a more elaborate apparatus than a single call; used honestly the hook obliges us to say what the extra passes buy that one pass does not.
