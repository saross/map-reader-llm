## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Liu and Hu — a two-author industry preprint (mycube.tv; arXiv v3, 13 November 2025) — reformulate referring expression comprehension (REC), the task of locating the one object a natural-language phrase describes, as box-wise true/false verification, and show that this workflow change, with no REC-specific training anywhere, beats both REC-trained GroundingDINO variants on most RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg splits. A generic detector (YOLO-World, deliberately trained without COCO) proposes boxes for a class the VLM itself names from the description; each proposal is re-rendered with only that box drawn, and the VLM answers a single binary question about it. Exactly one True returns that box; several True go to a second-stage selection over only those; no True falls back to a global selection over all boxes, with ‘none’ permitted as abstention (pages 1–2).

What makes this useful to us is a controlled ablation, not a leaderboard entry: same detector, same proposals, same VLM, only the prompting regime varies. Verification-first with GPT-5 reaches ACC@0.5 of 79.3, 74.2 and 72.4 on the three validation splits against 70.1, 66.7 and 69.7 for single-shot selection (page 3, Table II). The paper’s own summary of that gap — ‘about 5 to 10 absolute points across most of the splits’ — is properly hedged: our arithmetic puts the largest gaps at 12.1 points (both TestA columns) and the smallest at 2.7 and 3.1 (the two RefCOCOg splits).

The second finding is a null result that cuts against consensus-over-passes as we build it. Running the same selection prompt three times at temperature 1.0 and majority-voting moves 70.1 to 71.7, 66.7 to 67.4 and 69.7 to 69.9 — at most 1.6 points on each of the three validation splits where Table II reports it, for three times the calls. Their explanation: repeated calls to one VLM on one instance are rarely independent, so the votes reproduce each other; they then argue that verification-first is decorrelated by construction, each round querying a different region. That mechanism claim lands squarely on our repeated proposer passes: what resampling buys depends on whether the passes are genuinely different draws, not merely repeated ones. They also refuse the naive cost comparison, noting that majority voting needs three rounds where verification-first, for large q (their verifier’s true-positive rate), typically concludes in two.

Two limits travel with the enthusiasm. Off-the-shelf VLMs asked to emit a box directly are poor at it — ‘organic’ GPT-5 manages 10.1 to 12.5 across the three validation splits — and a verification stage can only prune, since performance is bounded by the generic detector’s proposal recall (page 0). ‘Zero-shot’ is defined narrowly and honestly: no REC training, a COCO-clean detector, but a VLM whose pretraining corpus ‘may include COCO images, which is not explicitly known’. The fully supervised CogVLM still leads on all eight splits, by 8.7 to 21.1 points on our arithmetic; and neither of the paper’s two averaged headlines — 12.3% over selection, 7.2% over REC-trained GroundingDINO — reproduces from Table II, with ‘%’ standing where the body says absolute points.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Primary cluster: open-vocabulary / VLM detection alternative — a zero-shot grounding pipeline assembled from a generic open-vocabulary detector and an off-the-shelf VLM, with no task-specific training anywhere, which is the closest published statement of our own bet that workflow design can substitute for fine-tuning. It doubles as a consensus / test-time-compute antecedent, and an awkward one: holding detector, proposals and VLM fixed, it shows independent per-candidate verification beating a joint selection prompt — the rationale for our adversarial verifier — while simultaneously demonstrating that majority voting over three repeated selection calls bought at most 1.6 points on each of the three validation splits where they ran it, the only splits Table II reports a majority-vote row for, which means our consensus-over-passes design has to be defended by a mechanism (decorrelation between passes) rather than by the fact of repetition. Its architecture also inverts ours: there the detector proposes and the VLM only judges, so the recall ceiling is external, whereas ours is the VLM’s own proposer recall and our verifier, like theirs, can only prune.
