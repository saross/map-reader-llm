## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Srivastava's working paper is the upstream node of the "adaptive preregistration" thread, and reading it settles D.9's naming question in a specific direction. The phrase is there — four occurrences: the abstract (page_index 2), the body sentence Gould et al. draw on (page_index 10), and a section heading plus a body clause (page_index 11) — but every instance outside the title-cased heading is a lowercase common noun ("adaptive preregistrations", "an adaptive preregistration"). It is never set in quotation marks as a coinage, formally defined, or claimed as a name. The concept Srivastava does define, and returns to throughout (thirty-seven occurrences), is decision independence: a result has it when the decisions that produced it "are not functions of unrepeatable features of the specific dataset being analyzed" (page_index 7). Adaptive preregistration, in his usage, is a mode of writing one — the thing his six strategies can be used to build — not a branded methodology.

The whole substance Gould et al. credit to him sits in a single sentence at page_index 10, and both of their components are relabellings: "registered flexibility" appears nowhere here (his phrase is "plans to deploy flexible strategies"), and he writes "interim registration", never "interim preregistration" (six occurrences against zero). So "sensu Srivastava, 2018" is fair as sense-attribution, while the two-component methodology is Gould et al.'s construction. Prior occupancy is real but thin, and sits in an unreviewed preprint ("This is a working paper.", draft date 28 November 2018).

Beyond naming, this source is closer to our study than Gould et al. are. It names machine learning among the paradigms where preregistration is hard (page_index 2, restated page_index 3), so a VLM evaluation sits inside its declared remit rather than in an untested extension. Table 1 (page_index 27) tabulates his six strategies by whether each can be preregistered and whether it helps with decisions arising after analysis has begun (for preregistration itself, "No"). Two bear directly on our design. Data partitioning is the strict form of our gold-standard-to-deployment transfer, whose rule is uncompromising: inferences come only from the holdout, with data leakage (citing Kaufman et al., 2012) named as the failure mode. The safeguard is two-part — the holdout plan registered before first contact with the data, then an interim registration written after training, before the holdout is touched — and recording carried operating points before a 55-map run matches only its second half. Multiverse analysis he offers as an after-the-fact diagnostic once decision independence has been lost, which is close to what our threshold sweeps supply retrospectively.

Caveats: nothing here is empirically evaluated — it is a conceptual review with psychology worked examples — and automation and language models go unmentioned, though preregistration websites and templates draw a passing mention (page_index 3, 8). One citation trap: the reference list carries a different Srivastava (2018), "Verifiability is a core principle of science", and page_index 21 cites that one, so a bare "Srivastava, 2018" is ambiguous between the two.

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "After training, the researcher can write an interim registration describing the decisions that were made during training and specifying the analysis that will be run in the holdout data"
- **Locator:** page_index 16 · p.17 · § Data Partitioning and Holdout Samples
- **Paraphrase:** The recommended sequencing for a partitioned design: once decisions have been settled on the training portion, write an interim registration that records those decisions and specifies the analysis to be run on the held-out data, before the holdout is touched. The immediately preceding sentence attributes the leakage guard to a different step: registering the holdout plan before first contact with the data, so that decisions about whether and how to use the holdout are made 'without conducting any analyses that included the held-out data, which can create data leakage (Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich, & Stitelman, 2012) and undermine decision independence'. The interim registration is the transparency half of the same combination — Srivastava credits the step to Santos & Grossman (2018) — not the leakage guard on its own.
- **Relevance:** § calibration transfer — recording carried operating points · Registered decision rules as procedural commitments · **supports**

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "Because of the risk of overfitting in training data, inferences are only made from the holdout data."
- **Locator:** page_index 15 · p.16 · § Data Partitioning and Holdout Samples
- **Paraphrase:** In the strict form of data partitioning, the holdout is analysed once, after every analysis decision is settled, and it is the only source of inference; results from the training partition are not inferential because of the risk of overfitting to it. The surrounding text scopes this to a partition set aside unanalysed before first contact with the data, and notes there is no simple rule for partition sizes.
- **Relevance:** § calibration transfer — carried vs oracle operating points · Calibration on a subset and leakage into evaluation · **complicates**

### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "A heuristic for thinking about decision independence is to consider a counterfactual: If the data were different, could the decisions have been different?"
- **Locator:** page_index 7 · p.8 · § Decision Independence Guards Against Overfitting
- **Paraphrase:** The operational test the whole framework rests on: ask whether different data could have produced different decisions; if not, the result has decision independence. Srivastava immediately qualifies it — decision independence is a safeguard, not a guarantee, is not the sole criterion for all analysis, and some data-dependent decisions (manipulation checks, residual inspection, model checking) are worth the added risk.
- **Relevance:** §D.9 — deviations and departures, stated criterion · Criterion for judging which of our data-dependent choices were legitimate · **supports**

- **Paraphrase:** When decisions had to be made that the registration did not anticipate, running the analysis across the full space of defensible specifications is offered as a retrospective diagnostic of how much the lost decision independence cost.
