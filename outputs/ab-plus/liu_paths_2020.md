## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Liu, Althoff and Heer interview the authors of nine published quantitative studies — five in human–computer interaction (HCI), two in proteomics, one in marine biology, one in geography — walking each through their own manuscript and analysis scripts in a three-phase protocol (free recall; brainstorming against a checklist based loosely on Wicherts et al.; comparison against alternatives the interviewers had prepared). The output is a three-theme taxonomy — six rationales for analytic decisions, four motivations for executing alternatives, four motivations for selective reporting — plus Analytic Decision Graphs, a notation for a study's decision points and the dataflow, procedural, information and feedback relations between them. Read it as the empirical portrait of the condition our registration answers, not an evaluation of the remedy: their participants "followed a preselected plan akin to pre-registration", but none of the nine studies was officially preregistered, and preregistration-then-exploration practices were "not observed" in the sample.

Two findings do real work for §D.9. First, the line between legitimate and illegitimate multi-path execution does not fall on whether alternatives were run but on what the selection was made against: systematicity is told from opportunism by the evaluation metric not hinging on anticipated conclusions — "the metric was not the end result". Carried across to our setting — the transfer is ours, not theirs; they consider nothing resembling a detection sweep — that licenses our threshold sweeps and operating-point selection exactly to the extent that the selection metric was fixed before the conclusion was: what registration buys and a timestamped deviations log evidences. Second, the failure is granular rather than gross: participants "did not recognize these steps as decision points and implicitly chose a single viable option". That is the grain-not-volume diagnosis from the other side — coverage gaps sit where nobody saw a fork. They also measure the scale: across the nine participants the multiverse runs from 16 to over 25,000,000 specifications, median 1,632.

One hygiene trap. The percentages in Table 1 are within-theme shares of coded instances, not participant prevalence: the denominators are 190 decision-rationale, 44 alternative-execution and 52 selective-reporting instances, glossed as "the ratio of unique instances within each theme", and the authors state their goal is "to characterize the space of analytic processes and decisions, not to quantify the prevalence of any specific activity" on a convenience, HCI-heavy sample. "Opportunism 45%" therefore means 20 of 44 coded instances (7/9 participants), nothing more — the same class of denominator slip as reading a class-relative F1 as an aggregate one.

Finally, their design agenda partly anticipates ours — analysis linters and recommenders, provenance tracking yielding "some level of automated documentation" of executed code paths, multiverse tooling — so the automation cell is at least gestured at in 2020, and a novelty claim for LLM-supported registration should be scoped to execution rather than to the idea. Their sociotechnical warning tempers it further: if publication incentives and reviewer criteria do not move, tools that reveal problematic choices "may be abandoned in favor of the status quo".

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

A canonical empirical characterisation of the problem our preregistration apparatus exists to answer, and the source that supplies the criterion by which our own multi-path exploration is defensible: nine unregistered analyses, taxonomised, with the forking-paths scale measured (16 to over 25,000,000 specifications, median 1,632) rather than asserted. Primary cluster: preregistration and open-science methodology, approached from the descriptive side — it says what unconstrained analytic freedom actually looks like, and does not test preregistration, since none of its nine studies was officially preregistered. It cuts both ways for §D.9: their systematicity/opportunism line, applied by us well beyond their setting, is the criterion on which registered sweeps whose selection metric is not the conclusion are defensible, while their call for automated decision documentation and their warning that such tools get abandoned when incentives do not move both temper the LLM-support novelty claim.
