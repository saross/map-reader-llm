## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Gerasimova proposes two additions to the argument-based approach to validity, and 'proposes' is load-bearing: neither is tested. The first is a living document — seven columns (Claim, Interpretation/Use, Evidence Needed, Validation Study Preregistration Link, Evidence Obtained, Conclusion, Date) plus an Overall Judgment summary row (page_index 14) — holding the whole validity argument in one place and updated as evidence arrives. The second splits preregistration in two: a single preregistration of the argument covering the entire validation process, which is literally that table's first four columns, and a conventional per-study preregistration nested beneath it and reached through the fourth column, with Claims and Evidence Needed required to be worded there exactly as in the argument preregistration (page_index 9). That two-tier shape is, on our reading, the closest structural analogue in this cluster to a phased registration: one durable top-level commitment to what would have to be true, plus study-level registrations that arrive as the programme proceeds.

Three mechanisms transfer nearly intact. First, the argument preregistration is explicitly mutable — claims and interpretations/uses can be added, modified, or removed in a transparent way during validation (page_index 8) — with a hard marking rule as the guardrail: updates made after evidence is in hand must be marked as made after seeing the evidence. Read across to our apparatus — the mapping is ours, not hers — that is a deviations log stated as a rule. Second, every substantive update spawns a new version and all versions should stay accessible (page_index 10) — which on our mapping does the work of an errata log, though her trigger is broader: a version is spawned by every addition of evidence or judgement, not only by a correction. Third, the living document may exist without any preregistration, and claims or evidence criteria may be specified retrospectively provided their retrospective nature is transparently reported (page_index 12). Read across, that licenses the registered-versus-added-later distinction an analyses register draws, without treating post hoc analyses as contraband.

Two cautions travel with it. The register is educational and psychological measurement: validity is a property of score interpretations and uses rather than of instruments, claims are propositions that must hold for a proposed use, and Evidence Needed is a criterion specified in advance (in her example, cut-off values) against which Evidence Obtained is compared to reach a Conclusion (page_index 9, 13). Read across, F1 and MCC on a gold standard are evidence for a claim, not the claim; what gets justified or withheld is the interpretation-and-use statement. And nothing is demonstrated: the only worked instance is a fictional high-school mathematics self-efficacy instrument, six claims across three planned validation studies, which the author says is 'not a full, and certainly not an exemplar, validity argument' (page_index 12); the template is called initial, with future research needed to evaluate its usability and usefulness (page_index 15). She names the infrastructure gap as well — registries have traditionally hosted preregistrations rather than updatable living documents (page_index 15) — and offers Registered Reports as the next step.

Its two limits are worth naming in the citing text: nothing is demonstrated beyond a fictional example the author declines to call exemplary, and the mechanism is written for score-validity claims, so the translation from 'Evidence Needed' to a detection metric at a chosen operating point is ours to make and to defend.

## Key points (salience-ranked to the citing paper)

### KP1
- **Quote (verbatim):** "Specifically, I propose two types of preregistration: preregistration of the argument and preregistration of each validation study. Preregistration of the argument is a single preregistration that is developed for the entire validation process."

- **Paraphrase:** Registration is split across two tiers: one argument-level preregistration written once for the whole validation programme, and a separate conventional preregistration for each validation study within it. The argument-level tier fixes interpretations, uses, and claims before any evidence is collected; the study tier fixes design, data collection, and analysis before each study's data collection, and the two are cross-linked (page_index 9).
- **Relevance:** § preregistration apparatus — phased design · Phased/adaptive registration: the registration unit and its tiers · **supports**

### KP2
- **Quote (verbatim):** "If the updates to any of the three columns are made after obtaining evidence, the developer must mark the changes as being made after seeing the evidence."
- **Locator:** page_index 8 · p.9 · § Developing a Living Document and Incorporating Preregistration in the Argument-Based Approach to Validity
- **Paraphrase:** Changes to the registered content (Claim, Interpretation/Use, Evidence Needed) are permitted mid-programme — the same paragraph says they may be added, modified, or removed in a transparent way — but any change made once evidence is in hand must be labelled as post-evidence. The obligation is to disclose the timing of a change, not to avoid changing.
- **Relevance:** § deviations and errata logs · Deviation reporting: marking post-evidence changes · **supports**

### KP3
- **Quote (verbatim):** "Notably, each time when Evidence Obtained, Conclusion, or Overall Judgment are added, a new version of the living document should be created. All versions should be accessible to readers."
- **Locator:** page_index 10 · p.11 · § Developing a Living Document and Incorporating Preregistration in the Argument-Based Approach to Validity
- **Paraphrase:** Each substantive addition of evidence or judgement produces a new numbered version of the document, and superseded versions are retained and remain reachable by readers rather than being overwritten. The same requirement is stated for the argument preregistration (page_index 9).
- **Relevance:** § deviations and errata logs · Errata log: versioned, publicly retained revisions · **supports**

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "when including historical evidence and retrospectively specifying corresponding Claim, Interpretation/Use, and/or Evidence Needed, their retrospective nature must be transparently reported."
- **Locator:** page_index 12 · p.13 · § Developing a Living Document and Incorporating Preregistration in the Argument-Based Approach to Validity
- **Paraphrase:** Retrospectively written claims and evidence criteria are admissible — the same paragraph says the living document could, strictly speaking, exist without preregistration at all, and that there is no reason to exclude evidence merely because it is historic — on the condition that their retrospective status is disclosed. Registered and after-the-fact content may coexist in one document provided the reader can tell which is which.
- **Relevance:** § analyses register · Analyses register: registered versus retrospectively specified analyses · **extends**

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "Making this judgment involves comparing two columns—Evidence Needed and Evidence Obtained. If Evidence Obtained matches Evidence Needed, then the developer concludes that the claim is supported."
- **Locator:** page_index 9 · p.10 · § Developing a Living Document and Incorporating Preregistration in the Argument-Based Approach to Validity
- **Paraphrase:** The verdict on a claim is a comparison against a criterion written down beforehand, not a fresh judgement made once the results are in: matched criterion means supported, unmatched means not supported. The source allows a partial or mixed verdict where several criteria are specified and only some are met (page_index 10), and asks that the criterion be specific, precise, and exhaustive — in the worked example, explicit cut-off values (page_index 13). The criterion is revisable rather than binding, though: Evidence Needed may be written up front or added study by study and updated later, post-evidence updates being marked (page_index 8), and where the developer deems the gap between Evidence Needed and Evidence Obtained small she permits updating Evidence Needed instead of recording non-support, provided the change is transparently reported (page_index 14). What the source guarantees is disclosure of a moved criterion, not a criterion that cannot move.
- **Relevance:** § methods — registered hypotheses and evaluation protocol · Registered decision rules: pre-specified evidence criteria · **complicates**

### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "I want to emphasize that the presented template of the living document (and of the preregistration of the argument) is initial, and future research is needed to evaluate its usability and usefulness."
- **Locator:** page_index 15 · p.16 · § Conclusion
- **Paraphrase:** The author states plainly that the template is a first version whose usability and usefulness remain to be evaluated, and asks developers to review, test, and adapt it. Nothing in the article is an executed application: the sole worked instance is a fictional instrument the author calls 'not a full, and certainly not an exemplar, validity argument' (page_index 12).
- **Relevance:** § discussion — positioning against registration prior art · Proposed versus demonstrated: how strong the prior-art claim actually is · **complicates**

### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "Traditionally, these registries have been limited to preregistrations rather than living documents (i.e., preregistrations that could be updated with obtained evidence and conclusions)."
- **Locator:** page_index 15 · p.16 · § Conclusion
- **Paraphrase:** Whether the living document and the argument preregistration can sit in one place is, on the author's account, a question of what public registries can do, and registries have conventionally hosted fixed preregistrations rather than documents updated with evidence and conclusions — though she notes the tradition may change in the future.
- **Relevance:** § discussion — where the registration apparatus lives · Registry and cyberinfrastructure limits · **complicates**

## Optional framing hook (not counted in the salience cap)

- **Quote (verbatim):** "the developer can rationalize an observed stability correlation that is lower than expected as still supporting the claim while believing that a lower threshold was the most reasonable from the beginning"
- **Locator:** page_index 5 · p.6
- **Why:** An epigraph for why an operating point has to be fixed before the results are seen: the failure mode is not dishonesty but a threshold that slides to meet the evidence while feeling as though it had always been set there. The next sentence supplies the empirical bite, reporting Watson (2004) as having found that researchers almost always labelled their stability correlations 'adequate' or 'satisfactory' irrespective of the size of those correlations.
