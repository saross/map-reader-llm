## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Wang et al. introduce self-consistency, the decoding strategy our consensus-over-passes stage descends from: prompt a model with chain-of-thought exemplars, sample diverse reasoning paths from its decoder instead of decoding greedily, then marginalise them away and keep the most frequent answer. The evidence base is text-only — arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning benchmarks over UL2-20B, LaMDA-137B, GPT-3-175B (code-davinci-001 and code-davinci-002) and PaLM-540B, plus five common NLP tasks on PaLM-540B (BoolQ, HotpotQA, e-SNLI, ANLI, RTE), all prompted without fine-tuning, few-shot throughout except one zero-shot chain-of-thought condition — so every transfer to vision-language detection is an extrapolation rather than a demonstration.

Four findings bear on our design. First, the mechanism rests on a stated hypothesis, not a proof: correct reasoning processes, even when diverse, are held to agree on their final answer more than incorrect ones do. Our greedy consensus inherits that assumption and its failure mode — a bias shared across passes concentrates rather than cancels.

Second, the authors bound their own method: self-consistency applies only where the final answer comes from a fixed answer set, and extends to open-text generation only if a good metric of consistency between multiple generations can be defined. Burial-mound detection has no fixed answer set — its answer is a variable-cardinality point set — and our spatial match radius is our candidate for the consistency metric the paper leaves undefined: the authors require a “good” metric and offer the extension only “in principle”.

Third, probability weighting bought them nothing. On PaLM-540B, unweighted majority vote scores 74.4 ± 0.1 on GSM8K against 74.1 ± 0.0 for the normalised weighted sum, because the model's normalised conditional probabilities sit close together — it regards the generations as similarly likely; a footnote concedes this means the model “is not well calibrated”. The “weighted average” variants fall to or below greedy decoding (56.3 and 22.1 against 56.5 on GSM8K). That supports greedy consensus as our primary aggregation and cautions against confidence-weighted variants.

Fourth, cost. The headline configuration is 40 sampled paths per question averaged over 10 runs, but the discussion recommends 5 or 10 paths as a practical starting point because performance saturates quickly (Figure 2 plots 1, 5, 10, 20 and 40 paths) — a published precedent for our K=10 arm, K=3 sitting below the smallest budget they recommend or plot.

Two secondary results matter. Consistency — the share of decodes agreeing with the aggregated answer — correlates with accuracy on GSM8K, making the agreement rate a free uncertainty signal beside verbalised confidence. And diversity is the active ingredient: beam search, which yields lower-diversity outputs, loses to sampling at every matched budget of 10 paths or more on UL2-20B (Table 6), though it still leads at one path and, on MultiArith, at five.

One reporting caution: the headline gains are absolute accuracy points over a greedy chain-of-thought baseline, and all five abstract deltas match the GPT-3 code-davinci-002 rows of Tables 2 and 3 (GSM8K's +17.9 also matches PaLM-540B), so they should never be quoted without their model and task.

## Optional framing hook (not counted in the salience cap)

- **Quote (verbatim):** "we hypothesize that correct reasoning processes, even if they are diverse, tend to have greater agreement in their final answer than incorrect processes"
- **Locator:** page_index 1 · p.2
- **Why:** The load-bearing assumption of every consensus-over-passes design, stated by its originators as a hypothesis rather than a result. It is a good hook because it makes the inheritance explicit and its limit audible in the same breath: agreement proxies for correctness only where errors are diverse. Where a model's errors are systematic — a map symbol that consistently reads as a mound across every pass — the premise fails and consensus concentrates the error instead of cancelling it, which is the gap our adversarial verifier stage is meant to cover.
