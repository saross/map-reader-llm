## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Zhang and colleagues add the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) to a YOLOv3 detector to recognise point symbols on scanned map sheets, then associate each recognised symbol with crawled point-of-interest (POI) records so that it can be placed, with its attributes, on a Cesium/Map World globe. Published in ISPRS International Journal of Geo-Information, it is — our judgement, not the paper's — the closest prior art to our task in object type: point symbols localised on a scanned raster map. That proximity is why its boundaries matter.

The corpus is three differently styled Chinese provincial atlases, scanned and labelled to PASCAL VOC format: 6,675 images, 12 symbol classes (school, hospital, market, government sector, bus station, mountain, post office, port, edifice, bank, PSB, hotel), 15,484 hand-drawn boxes, with the images split 5,406 / 601 / 668 across training, validation and test and no per-split box counts given. The paper never dates the atlases; the class inventory and the categorical pairing of recognised symbols with live AutoNavi POI records place them in contemporary urban cartography rather than a historical series — our inference, not a claim of the source. The reported operating point is 97.06% precision, 99.72% recall and 99.50% mAP, against Faster RCNN at 91.29 / 99.94 / 99.01 and SSD at 94.23 / 92.93 / 97.44; the CBAM gain over plain YOLOv3 is 0.55 points of mAP (98.95 → 99.50), reported without confidence intervals or repeated-seed variation.

Three qualifications should travel with those numbers. First, no intersection-over-union threshold is stated for mAP, the confidence threshold the precision and recall formulas invoke is never given, and the paper does not say which split Tables 3 and 4 report. Second, its definitions make both true and false positives counts of ground-truth boxes, so as written a detection matching no ground-truth object is not penalised; the paper links no implementation and offers data only on request, so whether the code follows the text is not checkable. Third, the running text restates the CBAM gain as 'reaching 99.36%' where Table 3, Table 4, the abstract and the conclusions all give 99.50% — an internal slip that should not be quoted.

The decisive sentences for us are in the conclusions. Unlabelled symbol types cannot be recognised at all, and extension to other national maps proceeds 'by making different datasets': the vocabulary is closed at annotation time and transfer is by re-annotation, because symbol designs for like features vary between national cartographic traditions. That is the shape of the supervised alternative our zero-training, prompted approach is positioned against — some 15,000 hand-drawn boxes over twelve classes of regular symbols on clean modern urban sheets, against one symbol family on cluttered Soviet-era topographic maps and no training corpus at all. The near-ceiling scores are not evidence that our task is easy; they are evidence that theirs was — as the paper says itself, map point symbols are regular shapes little affected by background information.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Primary cluster: historical-map extraction lineage — but at its modern, fully supervised end. Zhang et al. do the same shaped job we do (find and localise point symbols on a scanned map sheet, then attach semantics to what is found) and they do it well, which makes them the cleanest available statement of what our approach must clear: a closed vocabulary fixed at annotation time by 15,484 hand-drawn boxes over 12 classes, on three styles of Chinese provincial atlas — undated in the paper, but contemporary on the evidence of its symbol inventory — whose symbols the authors themselves describe as regular shapes barely affected by background. Secondarily it is an annotation-budget and metric-hygiene source: the 99.50% mAP headline arrives with no stated intersection-over-union threshold, no stated confidence threshold, no stated evaluation split and a definition of false positives that counts ground-truth boxes, so it can be cited as an existence proof for supervised symbol detection but not as a number ours is directly comparable with.
