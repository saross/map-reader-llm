## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Groot and Valdenegro-Toro ask one narrow question: if a model is told to append a confidence percentage to every answer, is that number worth anything? They test four LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, LLaMA-2-70b, and PaLM 2) on three NLP tasks — sentiment analysis in binary and float variants, GSM8K math word problems, CoNLL-2003 named-entity recognition on English sentences only, 100 randomly drawn samples per dataset — though the design is not a full four-by-three: LLaMA-2-70b and PaLM 2 could not do NER from a single instruction prompt and were dropped from that task (page_index 3). Two VLMs (GPT-4V and Gemini Pro Vision) were tested on Japanese Uncertain Scenes (JUS), a purpose-built set of 39 privately sourced photographs with deliberately hard or unanswerable prompts: count the birds, say where the photo was taken. The methodological contribution is the Net Calibration Error (NCE), a signed variant of expected calibration error.

The result that matters to us is Table 3: on JUS, Gemini Pro Vision answered at 88.4 per cent mean confidence while scoring 50.0 per cent accuracy — ECE 38.4, NCE -38.4 — against GPT-4V at 62.6 per cent confidence, 51.2 per cent accuracy and ECE 11.3. That is the model family we use, declaring near-certainty while right about half the time, on a counting-and-recognition task not far in kind from asking how many mound symbols a tile contains — though the authors' own scope note attaches: JUS exists 'not to assess the accuracy of specific models but to compare their calibration levels' (page_index 12), so 50.0 per cent is a calibration reference point, not a benchmark score for VLM counting. The generational caveat travels with every citation: this is Gemini Pro Vision as it stood in December 2023, several releases behind the models we prompt — evidence for a prior, not a measurement of our system.

Three further findings shape design, not just caution. First, the direction of miscalibration is task-dependent — underconfidence on sentiment analysis, overconfidence on maths and NER — so a correction fitted on one task cannot be assumed to carry to another, structurally the hazard our carried-versus-oracle operating points face when a gold-standard threshold goes to a 55-map deployment. Second, on maths and NER the stated confidences pile into the 90-100 per cent bin, leaving almost nothing to rank by and, the authors note, some bins sparsely populated. Third, the authors themselves ask whether GPT-4's better calibration is a capability or a byproduct of its higher accuracy.

Two things they lacked, we have. Each task ran once, so nothing here separates a confident answer from a stable one; consensus over repeated passes yields an agreement frequency the model does not author about itself. And their most encouraging VLM observation — GPT-4V returning 0 per cent confidence and no answer on a photograph the authors judge uncountable, where Gemini Pro Vision returned a count at 80 per cent — is precisely the abstention an adversarial verifier stage is meant to elicit, and it belonged to the other model family.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Primary cluster: calibration and verbalised confidence — this is the closest published measurement we can point to of whether a Gemini-family vision model's stated confidence means anything, and the answer is that it is badly inflated: 88.4 per cent mean confidence against 50.0 per cent accuracy on the authors' own image set. Whether anything usable survives as a ranking signal is a different question, and this paper does not settle it — its only direct test of whether that model's confidence tracks correctness is a per-bin Pearson correlation of accuracy against mean confidence (Appendix E, Table 7, page_index 12), and the authors report that those correlation tables 'mostly show high p-values', which they attribute to 'the relatively low number of confidence bins that contained any data points' — underpowered rather than negative. It is a prior rather than a result about our pipeline, since the model is Gemini Pro Vision as tested in December 2023 and the substrate is holiday photographs rather than map tiles; cited honestly it motivates a design decision rather than settling one — why our detection score comes from agreement across passes and an adversarial verifier rather than from a number the proposer writes about itself. It touches two other clusters secondarily: methods/metrics, through the signed-versus-absolute calibration error and the sparse-bin ECE problem, and consensus / test-time compute, where the paper's single-run-per-task design is exactly the gap repeated sampling fills.

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "the model is aware of its inability to provide the correct answer, and therefore outputs 0% confidence and does not give an answer to the question, showing perfect calibration. - **Paraphrase:** On a photograph whose reference answer is 'Over 100 birds, hard to count the exact number' (page_index 1), GPT-4V declined to give a count and returned 0 per cent confidence, while Gemini Pro Vision returned '100' at 80 per cent confidence.
