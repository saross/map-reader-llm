## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Zhou and colleagues (NeurIPS 2024) ask where the instability of in-context learning (ICL) lives, and answer: inside the model. Working with Llama-2 7B, they project individual feedforward network (FFN) value vectors and attention heads into vocabulary space through the logit lens, showing that three named bias factors have identifiable internal signatures — vanilla label bias in accumulated uncontextual FFN logits, recency bias in an attention head (layer 16, head 29) that favours the final exemplar regardless of the true label, and selection bias towards option "A" in multiple-choice items. UniBias then masks the components meeting three criteria (relatedness, bias, low variance). It is inference-only, though the threshold grid search is an up-front cost, and needs white-box access — putting the cure, not the diagnosis, out of reach for an API-served Gemini.

Three things earn it a place in our few-shot section. First, the magnitude of the brittleness documented: standard one-shot ICL accuracy varies by 8% to 26% under changes of exemplar choice, order, or prompt template alone (SST-2 for selection and formatting, AGNews for order, Llama-2 7B), against under 4% after debiasing. That is design-setting variance with the task held fixed — not the sampling variance our consensus-over-passes design reduces, but the strongest external argument we have for treating prompt design as a measurable source of variance.

Second, the common-components experiment (§4.4, Table 2) is a rough analogue of our carried-versus-oracle operating points — rough because the paper never says the six evaluation datasets were held out of the frequency counts that produced the common set. A fixed set of eight commonly-biased attention heads, applied across six datasets rather than tuned per task, recovers most of the per-task gain: 70.84 (standard ICL) → 74.84 (full UniBias) → 73.98 (fixed common set), a gap of 0.86 on a 4.00-point gain on our arithmetic, not the paper's — roughly four-fifths retained. Only part of that gap is transfer: the common set masks attention heads alone where full UniBias also masks FFN vectors, and Table 3's attention-only row over the same six datasets averages 74.54, so about 0.30 of it is the FFN ablation and about 0.55 the move from per-task to fixed identification. What remains concentrates in one dataset (TREC loses 3.20, MR gains 0.24) and is smaller than the five-repetition standard deviations in either row. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude reference point for a carried operating point's cost, not a measured rate.

Third, calibration is not free and can be negative. Prototypical Calibration averages 66.81 against standard ICL's 67.07 on Llama-2 7B, collapsing on ARC (40.40 against 51.90) and MMLU (34.12 against 41.73); Domain-Context Calibration on 13B (72.08) likewise sits below vanilla ICL (72.54). PC's numbers are a modified reproduction, though: Appendix A.2 substitutes 200 training samples per class for the original's test samples as the estimate set, so DC — no recorded deviation — carries the point more cleanly. A correction chosen on one distribution can be worse than none on another.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Few-shot / in-context learning and annotation budget is the primary cluster: this is the mechanistic account of why ICL is prompt-brittle, and the sharpest quantification available to us of how far accuracy moves when only the prompt's design settings change (8% to 26% on one-shot Llama-2 7B, under 4% after debiasing). Its cure is closed to us — masking FFN vectors and attention heads needs white-box access an API-served Gemini does not offer — so we cite it for the diagnosis and for two further results: a single fixed set of biased components, applied uniformly instead of re-identified per task, retains most of the per-task gain (74.84 against 73.98 over six datasets), though part of that gap is an FFN-masking ablation rather than a transfer cost and the paper does not show the fixed set was chosen on held-out tasks; and output-side calibration can be worse than none (Domain-Context Calibration at 72.08 against standard ICL's 72.54 on Llama-2 13B). Secondarily it feeds the consensus and test-time-compute thread, but as an adjacent quantity rather than the same one: the variance UniBias removes is variance across prompt designs, whereas consensus over repeated passes at a fixed prompt reduces sampling variance — so the paper motivates our concern with output stability without measuring what our architecture buys.

### KP3
- **Quote (verbatim):** "In contrast to existing works based on external adjustments of LLM outputs, we mitigate LLM bias through manipulation of LLM internal structure."
- **Locator:** page_index 1 · p.2 · §1 Introduction — contributions
- **Paraphrase:** The paper defines itself against the calibration family that adjusts model outputs, and intervenes on the model's internals instead. That boundary is the applicability boundary for us: the method is proposed for decoder-only LLMs (page_index 4) and implemented by masking attention heads and adjusting FFN coefficients through hook operations (page_index 13), none of which is available through a hosted vision-language model API. Of our own mitigations, threshold calibration on a gold standard sits squarely in the family the authors are arguing past — which they define narrowly, as calibration methods that counteract bias "by adjusting decision boundaries of model output probabilities" (page_index 0) — while repeated sampling with consensus and an adversarial verifier pass are interventions this paper neither tests nor discusses. ### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "when comparing the labels 'good' and 'bad', the difference in their uncontextual accumulated FFN logits is more pronounced than that between 'positive' and 'negative,' resulting in a larger discrepancy in prediction frequency"
- **Locator:** page_index 3 · p.4 · §2.2 Internal Mechanisms of Bias Factors — Vanilla Label Bias
- **Paraphrase:** The model's prior over answer tokens is a property of the words chosen to name the classes, not of the input: swapping 'positive' / 'negative' for 'good' / 'bad' widens the imbalance in accumulated uncontextual FFN logits and correspondingly widens the imbalance in how often each label is predicted, and the pair 'satisfied' / 'disappointed' reverses the direction of the effect. Scope: Llama-2 7B, sentiment analysis, prediction frequencies measured zero-shot on SST-2. The mechanism is offered as an explanation of vanilla label bias, not as a controlled experiment on label wording.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — prompt design and the wording of the decision · Prompt wording as a source of prior — label-name choice · **complicates**

### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "the biased attention head (layer 16, head 29) consistently assigns significantly larger attention weights to the final example, irrespective of the ground truth labels of the test samples"
- **Locator:** page_index 3 · p.4 · §2.2 Internal Mechanisms of Bias Factors — Recency Bias
- **Paraphrase:** Recency bias — the pull towards the label of the last exemplar in the prompt — is localised to particular attention heads, one of which keeps favouring the final example even when the exemplar order is reversed, whereas a comparison head in the same layer splits its attention almost evenly. The demonstration is deliberately small: SST-2, one positive and one negative exemplar, 40 test samples evenly split, Llama-2 7B, with attention summed over label words rather than whole examples (page_index 13). It gives a mechanism for the exemplar-order sensitivity that any few-shot prompt of ours inherits.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — exemplar selection and ordering in the proposer prompt · Exemplar order sensitivity in few-shot prompting · **supports**
