## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Fafchamps and Labonne propose a procedure sitting between a preanalysis plan (PAP) and replication. An independent third party randomly draws two non-overlapping subsets; the researcher analyses the training half freely, revising in response to seminars, editors, and referees; on acceptance, that methodology runs unchanged on the testing half, and those results are published. The claim is that this beats a PAP on power in empirically relevant settings, by a mechanism the abstract names: the effect "mostly operates through a lower likelihood that relevant hypotheses are left untested" — a PAP lists only hypotheses thought of in advance.

Every headline number is conditional. The baseline simulation sets m = 100 candidate null hypotheses of which m0 = 99 are true, a 50–50 split (s = 0.5), Bonferroni corrections, and α = 5%; the PAP arm is assumed to test only 10 of those 100 (page_index 7). At a standardised effect size of 0.2, split-sample power is "well above 0.8" once N reaches 3,000; at effect size 0.1 it takes 10,000 or more (page_index 7 and 8 respectively). Dominance over a PAP turns on ψ, the chance the relevant nontrue hypothesis is in the plan: the split wins once N exceeds 700 at ψ = 0.25, 1,500 at 0.50, and 2,300 at .75 (page_index 7). Their Type I claim is narrower than it looks and does not rest on Table 1, which puts family-wise error below 5% under both methods and reads 0.048 for both at the headline parameterisation (page_index 11) — parity, not advantage; superiority is argued instead on referee degrees of freedom (page_index 13).

Two features sharpen the comparison with our calibrate-then-deploy design. Their confirmation half is a random split, and that is the appeal: the data "come from the same context and were collected using the same protocols". Our transfer is not that — the 55-map deployment is a different sample of sheets, so the transfer tax we measure carries distribution shift this framework excludes by construction. And their commitment rests on custody: the method is "most useful for situations where researchers can credibly commit not to have seen the data before sending to a third party" (page_index 13). We had no custodian, and we built the gold standard ourselves.

What they give the preregistration retrospective is a 2017 precedent for the move itself: register after exploring, and bind the registration to data you have not seen. The S139 scout filed them under outcome-blindness *without* registration, which undersells them: the accepted paper is "akin to" a detailed PAP — a registration, just a late one. What they do not give is grain (one split, one registration point), automation, or a working mechanism: they call for a professional association to host the platform, rule post-publication reanalysis out "by construction" while classing publicly available data and own-lab experiments only as "not, at this point, implementable in a fully credible manner", with remedies named for both (split-ready new data rounds; laboratory embargoes), and concede they have not shown their method undominated.
