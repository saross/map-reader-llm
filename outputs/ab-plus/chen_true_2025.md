## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Chen and colleagues (COLM 2025) make a diagnostic argument bearing directly on any decision to spend annotation budget on few-shot image exemplars: multimodal large language models given image-text demonstrations largely do not look at the demonstration images. They trace this to imbalanced attention allocation between visual and textual tokens, and offer two artefacts — DARA, a fine-tuning method that scales the attention-score columns belonging to visual tokens, and TrueMICL, a benchmark of seven tasks in four categories built so the query is unanswerable without the demo images. The remedy is white-box and so unavailable to us: DARA edits attention inside an open-weights backbone, here Qwen2-VL-7B, Idefics3-8B and Phi-3.5-Vision (4.2B). The diagnosis and the evaluation design are what transfer.

Two results matter most. First, the negative control. On standard vision-language benchmarks, zero-shot, text-only demonstrations, and full 4-shot image-text demonstrations are near indistinguishable — Qwen2-VL on VQAv2 scores 78.6, 78.7 and 79.1 respectively. If a few-shot condition in our design shows a gain, we cannot credit it to the exemplar images without running the text-only-demonstrations arm — the same ablation logic as our text-versus-image modality comparison, aimed at the demonstrations rather than the query. Second, curating better exemplars converts unevenly. Retrieval-based selection (RICES: the four most similar support images per query) moves Qwen2-VL's TrueMICL average from 77.67 to 79.71, Idefics3 from 41.95 to 43.43 and Phi-3.5-Vision from 44.62 to 47.05. That is an annotation-budget finding with a split implication: the average gain is small because exemplar relevance buys little on the math-reasoning, concept-binding and pattern-finding tasks, while on the novel-concept task (Character Classification: +10.67, +9.33 and +16.67 points) it converts strongly in all three models. Exemplar curation pays where the model must bind an unfamiliar visual category to a new label, which is the closest analogue in this benchmark to learning an unfamiliar map symbol.

The crucial scoping caveat runs the other way and must travel with every use of this paper. GPT-4o, the one frontier closed-source model tested, moves from failing most tasks at 0-shot (Operator 8.00, Clock 2.00, CLEVR 4.00, though Sudoku 94.00) to at or near ceiling at 4-shot (100.00, 87.00, 96.00). The pessimistic headline is attested on 4.2B–8.5B open-weights models, not on this one, whose gain the authors read as showing the context images to be indispensable — though no text-only-demonstration arm was run for GPT-4o to isolate them. Our proposer is Gemini, a frontier family, so this paper obliges us to run the ablation rather than predict that few-shot prompting will not help.

Two smaller things. Returns to shot count are bounded: gains hold only within a range the authors illustrate as up to ten shots for Qwen2-VL, bounded by how many images the model reliably processes at once, beyond which neither DARA nor LoRA guarantees further gains. And rewriting the minimal prompt into an explicit instructional one moved Qwen2-VL by at most one accuracy point across six tasks — a null result under fixed demonstrations. Several internal number inconsistencies are logged in the overflow notes.

### KP1
- **Quote (verbatim):** "they tend to neglect visual cues and over-rely on textual patterns, leading to mere text imitation rather than genuine multimodal adaptation. This behavior makes MICL still unimodal and largely restricts its practical utility."
- **Locator:** page_index 0 · p.1 · Abstract
- **Paraphrase:** The paper's core diagnosis: multimodal models given image-text demonstrations attend to the textual pattern and largely ignore the demonstration images, so what looks like multimodal in-context learning is text imitation. The claim is made in the abstract as a general statement about 'current MLLMs'; the evidence in the paper is from three open-weights models of 4.2B to roughly 8.5B parameters (Qwen2-VL, Idefics3, Phi-3.5-Vision), and the one frontier closed model tested behaves differently (see KP4).
- **Relevance:** §2 related work — what few-shot multimodal prompting actually buys · Few-shot / in-context learning / annotation budget — visual-context neglect in multimodal ICL · **complicates**

### KP2
- **Quote (verbatim):** "this disadvantage is hard to discern as it can be concealed by the improved performance on tasks where reasonable responses can be generated from text pattern following, without a deep understanding of visual context"
- **Locator:** page_index 1 · p.2 · §1 Introduction
- **Paraphrase:** Visual-context neglect is hard to detect because it hides behind genuine accuracy gains on tasks that can be solved by following the textual pattern alone. The paper's supporting instance is prior work (Chen et al., 2023b) finding that dropping the demo images from image captioning still yields comparable performance, from which the authors conclude in the next sentence that such datasets are not suitable for evaluating multimodal in-context learning at all.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — why a few-shot arm needs an image-ablated control · Methods/metrics — a few-shot gain is not evidence the exemplar images were used · **supports**

### KP3
- **Quote (verbatim):** "By comparing the first three settings, no demonstrations (Zero-shot), demonstrations without images (No-image), and random 4-shots, we observe almost no differences in performance. Taking Qwen2-VL on VQAv2 as an example, the accuracy under these settings is 78.6%, 78.7%, and 79.1%, respectively."
- **Locator:** page_index 7 · p.8 · §4.4 Result Analysis on Standard VL Datasets
- **Paraphrase:** The cheap diagnostic, run and reported: on a standard benchmark, zero-shot, text-only demonstrations and full image-text 4-shot demonstrations are effectively tied (78.6, 78.7, 79.1 for Qwen2-VL on VQAv2), so the demonstration images buy very little there — though 'little' is not 'nothing': across Table 8 the image-bearing 4-shot arm beats the no-image arm in all twelve model × dataset cells, by +0.4 to +2.2 accuracy points (+0.3 to +0.7 CIDEr on COCO), and Qwen2-VL on VQAv2, the example the paper picks, is the smallest of the accuracy cases at +0.4. The comparison is within-model and the spread is smaller than the gaps the paper treats as meaningful elsewhere; no confidence intervals accompany these standard-VL numbers (Table 8, page_index 18), unlike the TrueMICL results, which carry standard deviations over three seeds.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — modality ablation design · §5 discussion · Text-versus-image modality comparison — the no-image control as design pattern · **supports**

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "In the 0-shot setting, GPT-4o fails on most tasks except a few logic-based ones like Sudoku. With 4-shot demonstrations, it achieves significantly better accuracy (full results in Appendix D.2), highlighting the indispensable role of context images in TrueMICL. However, despite GPT-4o's strong performance, certain fundamental MICL challenges persist; for example, GPT-4o's accuracy significantly decreases when facing more challenging tasks such as our specially designed harder Sudoku variant."
- **Locator:** page_index 9 · p.10 · §4.5 Ablation Study — Evaluation on GPT-4o
- **Paraphrase:** The scoping caveat on the paper's own headline. The single frontier closed-source model tested goes from 0-shot failure on every task but Sudoku (94.00) to at or near ceiling with four demonstrations — Table 10 (page_index 19) reports Operator 8.00 to 100.00, Clock 2.00 to 87.00, Outlier 26.00 to 99.00, CLEVR 4.00 to 96.00, Sudoku 94.00 to 100.00, Palindrome 33.00 to 97.00, and the harder Sudoku variant 0.00 to 91.00. The authors keep two caveats attached: the harder Sudoku variant is where challenges persist, and being closed-source it is unclear whether the gains come from scale or from undisclosed architecture and training. n = 1 model, six tasks plus the hard variant — Character Classification, the novel-concept task, is not among them — and no seeds or intervals for this table. Most importantly, Table 10 runs only Zero-shot and 4-shot MICL: there is no No-image arm for GPT-4o, so by the very control logic this entry borrows from the paper (KP2, KP3) the jump cannot be credited to the demonstration images rather than to the demonstration text. The paper ran that arm for the three open-weights models and not for this one.
- **Relevance:** §2 related work — scoping borrowed ICL findings to the model class we actually use · Few-shot / in-context learning — does the neglect finding transfer to frontier models? · **complicates**

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "simply improving the relevance of the shots (as done in RICE) is insufficient to enhance model's performance further"
- **Locator:** page_index 5 · p.6 · §4.2 Result Analysis on TrueMICL
- **Paraphrase:** Retrieval-based exemplar selection — choosing the four most similar support images for each query rather than four random ones — buys little on tasks that genuinely require the demonstration images, because the bottleneck is the model's use of visual context rather than the exemplars' relevance. Scoped to these three open-weights models on TrueMICL at 4 shots: Table 2 (page_index 6) puts RICES against random at 79.71 versus 77.67 average for Qwen2-VL, 43.43 versus 41.95 for Idefics3 and 47.05 versus 44.62 for Phi-3.5-Vision, so 'insufficient' means small positive gains, not none — and the gains are almost entirely one task. Character Classification (novel concept) moves 83.33 to 94.00 for Qwen2-VL, supplying 1.52 of its 2.04-point average gain; 67.67 to 77.00 for Idefics3 (1.33 of 1.48, 90%); 68.00 to 84.67 for Phi-3.5-Vision (2.38 of 2.43, 98%). Across the other six tasks the RICES-minus-random deltas span -1.00 to +2.00. Retrieval therefore fails to convert on math reasoning, concept binding and pattern finding, but converts strongly, in all three models, on the one task that requires binding an unfamiliar visual category to a new label.
- **Relevance:** §5 discussion — where annotation effort pays · Annotation budget — does spending on exemplar curation convert? · **complicates**

### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "query questions can often be answered by either inspecting only the query image or imitating the answer style in the demo text, without utilizing the demonstration images"
- **Locator:** page_index 4 · p.5 · §3.2 TrueMICL, a MICL-dedicated Dataset
- **Paraphrase:** The authors' analysis of why existing multimodal ICL datasets fail as diagnostics: their queries are solvable from the query image alone or by copying the answer style of the demonstration text. Behind it sits the task-recognition versus task-learning split they borrow from Pan et al. (2023) — recognising a familiar task and applying pre-trained priors, versus acquiring a new input-label mapping from the demonstrations — with existing datasets focusing primarily on the first and largely overlooking the second. The same page adds the caveat that most helps our scoping: 'Task learning is scale-dependent and exhibits significant discrepancies across models of varying sizes (Pan et al., 2023)' — the source's own reason not to carry small-model pessimism to a frontier model.
- **Relevance:** §2 related work · §5 discussion — what our few-shot exemplars could be teaching · Difficulty ladder — where symbol detection sits between task recognition and task learning · **extends**

### KP7

The authors sweep 2 to 32 shots (Figure 4) and report that the improvement holds only within a range, illustrated as up to about ten shots for Qwen2-VL and attributed to how many images the model can reliably process at once; past that, neither of the fine-tuning methods guarantees further gains. - **Relevance:** §5 discussion — sizing a few-shot budget · Annotation budget — returns to more exemplars are bounded, not guaranteed · **complicates**

## Optional framing hook (not counted in the salience cap)

- **Quote (verbatim):** "1) Context dependency: The task must be unsolvable without the context images."
- **Locator:** page_index 4 · p.5
- **Why:** The cleanest one-line statement of the falsifiability standard we should hold our own few-shot condition to: an exemplar set earns its annotation cost only if removing the exemplar images breaks the task. Chen et al. state it as a dataset-construction principle; for us it inverts into an evaluation principle — run the no-image arm, and treat a few-shot gain that survives image removal as evidence of formatting or task-recognition benefit, not of visual learning. Use it with the caveat that it is a design goal the authors assert for TrueMICL, supported by their own zero-shot and no-image baselines and a 20-participant human study, not an audited property of every task: Qwen2-VL scores 88.00 zero-shot on Sudoku against a 36.38 zero-shot average, and GPT-4o 94.00; the authors do introduce a harder Sudoku variant, though they nowhere state that the zero-shot solvability of the original is the reason for it. See the overflow notes.

## Overflow (paraphrase only; each rests on a byte-checked span)

Verified secondary material that did not fit the summary band. The verbatim spans behind these paraphrases are held in the working copy (`_work/chen_true_2025.overflow.json`), not published; page anchors are to the extracted page cache (structured 2026-09-03; model `claude-opus-5` requested).

- **Transfer gains overstated** — Chen and colleagues summarise their cross-task transfer table as showing that DARA trained on one TrueMICL task gives 2–5% higher accuracy on tasks it was not trained on, but the same table holds cells at +0.00%, -0.23% and -0.33%, so the quoted range describes the better part of the distribution rather than its span. The arithmetic in one of those cells is also wrong: the Sudoku-trained row reports 95.67% (-0.23%) in the Palindrome column against a 96.00% baseline, a difference of -0.33%. The table covers Qwen2-VL only, across six of the seven tasks, with no seeds or intervals reported. (page_index 8 · p.9 · §4.5 Ablation Study — Transferability on untuned tasks (Table 3))
- **Two matching rules, one metric** — TrueMICL accuracy is not a single matching rule: numerical answers are scored by strict matching, textual answers by keyword matching, with manual verification applied to an unspecified subset of the predictions. The paper does not report how large that subset was, which tasks it covered, or what the manual pass changed. (page_index 16 · p.17 · Appendix C.2 Datasets and Evaluation Metrics (repeated at §4.1))
- **Averages are unweighted** — Every TrueMICL task has a 30-sample support set and a 100-query test set except Character Classification, whose test set is 50 because the character images came from movie scenes. The ‘Average’ column of the paper's main results table is therefore an unweighted mean over seven task accuracies with unequal denominators, not a sample-weighted accuracy, and should be quoted as a mean of task scores. (page_index 14 · p.15 · Appendix B TrueMICL)
- **Uncertainty reported unevenly** — Only the main TrueMICL results table reports variability, as standard deviations over three seeds. The cross-task transfer table, the standard vision-language table, the GPT-4o table and the prompt-design table are all bare point estimates, so the one- and two-point differences those tables carry come with no stated uncertainty. (page_index 6 · p.7 · §4.2 Result Analysis on TrueMICL — Table 2 caption)
- **Visual neglect, quantified** — The paper puts a number on its visual-neglect diagnosis: without DARA the model directs 28% of its attention to image tokens, rising to 46.7% once DARA is applied. This is a single normalised attention-allocation ratio read off one figure; the surrounding text does not say which of the three models it comes from, and no variability is reported. (page_index 6 · p.7 · §4.3 Visualization of the DARA's Reallocation Effects (Figure 2(b)))
- **Novel-concept task dropped** — Three of the paper's tables report only six of TrueMICL's seven tasks: the full-parameter LoRA versus LoRA+DARA comparison, the GPT-4o evaluation (Table 10, page 19) and the prompt-design ablation (Table 11, page 20). The task left out of all three is Character Classification, the novel-concept task on which retrieval-based exemplar selection and DARA both produce their largest gains, so it appears in no comparison outside the main results table. (page_index 18 · p.19 · Appendix D.1 — Table 9 caption)
- **Mixed metrics in one table** — The standard vision-language results table mixes metrics: accuracy for VQAv2, GQA and A-OKVQA, but CIDEr for MSCOCO. The COCO column's values of roughly 116 to 120 are on a different scale from the other three columns and must not be read as percentages. (page_index 18 · p.19 · Appendix D.1 — Table 8 caption)
- **Human study reported thinly** — The authors ran a human study with 20 participants, who generally failed the tasks when no demonstrations were given and improved once demonstrations were included. No accuracies, per-task breakdown, recruitment detail or comparison against the models is reported, so the study establishes that a human baseline was collected rather than supplying one that can be cited. (page_index 9 · p.10 · §4.5 Ablation Study — Human Evaluation)
- **Prompt rewording, full numbers** — The prompt-design ablation in full: rewriting Qwen2-VL's minimal prompt as an explicit instructional one moved accuracy from 67/31/87/86/94/96 to 67/30/87/87/95/96 across Operator, Clock, Outlier, CLEVR, Sudoku and Palindrome — two tasks up a point, one down a point, three unchanged. It is one model, one prompt pair and six tasks, reported as whole percentages with no seeds or intervals, so it bounds prompt-wording effects under fixed demonstrations rather than measuring them. (page_index 20 · p.21 · Appendix D.4 Effect of Prompt Design — Table 11)
- **Forcing attention destabilises** — A hard-coded alternative to DARA failed: masking the attention logits so that half the heads ignored text tokens entirely, forcing attention onto image tokens, produced unstable and incoherent outputs. The authors take the contrast as evidence that the benefit comes from a soft, learned rebalancing of attention rather than from simply pushing more attention onto the images. No accuracies are reported for this baseline. (page_index 9 · p.10 · §4.5 Ablation Study — Hard-coded Attention Adjustment)
- **Headline figures unrepresentative** — Both numbers in the paper's one-line summary of DARA sit outside what its body reports. The parameter count is configuration-dependent — page 5 works a four-shot, 32-head example to 5 × 32 = 160, and page 18 gives 140 in the Figure 5 caption and ‘fewer than 225’ in Appendix D.1 — so ‘around 100’ is below every configuration the paper actually reports. The largest gains also exceed 10 points: on Character Classification, DARA lifts Idefics3 from 67.67 to 90.67 and Phi-3.5-Vision from 68.00 to 93.00 over the random four-shot baseline. (page_index 1 · p.2 · §1 Introduction)
- **Benchmark size stated twice** — The benchmark's size is given inconsistently: §3.2 states 867 samples across four categories and seven tasks, while §1 (page 2) states 860. Table 1's per-task counts — 30 support and 100 test for every task except Character Classification at 30 and 50 — sum to 860, which is the figure the paper's own table supports. (page_index 4 · p.5 · §3.2 TrueMICL, a MICL-dedicated Dataset)
