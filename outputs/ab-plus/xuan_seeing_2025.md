## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Xuan and colleagues (EMNLP 2025 main conference) evaluate verbalised confidence across three model categories — general instruction-tuned, text-centric reasoning, and vision-centric reasoning (“thinking with images”) — four task domains, and three evaluation scenarios: a general setting (image plus text instruction), an embedded-instruction setting with the question rendered inside the image, and a semantically aligned setting serving identical content as image or as text. Confidence is elicited by the mechanism a prompt-driven detector has available — a 0 to 100 per cent score requested inside a confidence tag on a chain-of-thought prompt — and scored with Expected Calibration Error (ECE) over ten bins. On our reading it is the closest published evidence on whether a vision-language model's stated confidence can carry weight in a detection pipeline; the verdict is mostly negative.

Three results bear directly on our design. First, miscalibration is the default and it is directional: most instruction-tuned and text-reasoning models exceed ECE 0.25 in the majority of settings (page_index 3), and on the paper's one calibration curve (Figure 2, six models on the MathVision testmini split) the authors report systematic overconfidence across a wide range of confidence bins (page_index 4). Second, reasoning modality matters more than reasoning mode: on MMMU-Pro (Standard, 10) o3 records ECE 0.047 against 0.496 for Qwen2.5-VL 7B, whereas setting an instruction-tuned model beside a similarly scaled text-reasoning one buys much less (Kimi-VL-A3B Instruct 0.492 to Thinking 0.433; GPT-4.1 0.321 against o1's 0.245) (page_index 4) — a comparison confounded with vendor, since both vision-reasoning models are closed OpenAI systems (page_index 3, 8). Third, and most useful for our text-versus-image comparison, the modality gap is a calibration gap: on IsoBench, with content held constant, Qwen2.5-VL 72B scores ECE 0.431 on images against 0.200 on text and InternVL3 78B 0.412 against 0.198 (page_index 5) — though o3's aggregate runs the other way (0.037 image, 0.081 text).

Their remedy, Visual Confidence-Aware Prompting, is a two-round split worth reading as a staged-prompting antecedent — not a verification one, since round two answers the task rather than checking a candidate output: round one asks only for a description of the image and a confidence in that description, which round two then conditions on. On IsoBench it lowers All-domain ECE from 0.431 to 0.365 (Qwen2.5-VL 72B) and 0.412 to 0.331 (InternVL3 78B) with accuracy holding or rising, but the margin over a plain self-reflection baseline is uneven — 0.012 to 0.066 ECE across the four open models, and reversed on o4-mini (0.095 against 0.089) (page_index 7) — against the authors' own claim of "significant gains" over that baseline (page_index 1).

Two hygiene points travel with the numbers. ECE on the factuality datasets is computed over attempted questions only, and the fewer than 2 per cent of responses carrying no confidence at all were excluded rather than imputed (page_index 3, 34). And although the paper names failure prediction alongside calibration, only ECE reaches the main tables; AUROC sits in the appendix, where low ECE and usable ranking repeatedly come apart.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Primary cluster: calibration and verbalised confidence. This is the reference evaluation for the elicitation mechanism a prompt-driven detector has available — a stated score inside a tag — and its finding is that the raw number is untrustworthy as a probability while remaining stable enough in aggregate to be reported, though that stability evidence is three models, three runs, and one benchmark (page_index 35). It bears secondarily on the consensus and test-time-compute thread, since its two-stage VCAP prompt decomposes perception from task execution and carries a first-round confidence into the second (with modest, condition-specific gains) — a weaker relative of our proposer-then-verifier split, whose second stage checks rather than answers — and on our text-versus-image modality comparison, for which it supplies the sharpest available result: with content held constant, the image side is the badly calibrated side. It should be read as a constraint rather than a licence — every task here is multiple-choice or short-answer benchmark work, not localisation, so any use we make of a per-detection confidence needs its own calibration evidence.

- **Quote (verbatim):** "VCAP separates visual understanding from task execution to improve calibration accuracy."
- **Locator:** page_index 6 · p.7 · §5 VISUAL CONFIDENCE-AWARE PROMPTING (VCAP)
- **Paraphrase:** Visual Confidence-Aware Prompting splits the interaction in two: round one asks only for a detailed description of the image plus a confidence score for that description; round two performs the task, conditioned on the round-one description and its confidence. On IsoBench the All-domain ECE falls from 0.431 to 0.365 for Qwen2.5-VL 72B and from 0.412 to 0.331 for InternVL3 78B, with accuracy rising rather than being traded away for these two (53.7 to 57.0; 54.1 to 56.0) (page_index 7), though the authors note that "moving beyond vanilla prompting often entails an accuracy penalty" (page_index 35) and o4-mini does lose accuracy under VCAP (78.1 to 77.4). ### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "We examine the use of confidence scores from LLMs: calibration and failure prediction."
- **Locator:** page_index 3 · p.4 · §3.3 Evaluation Metrics
- **Paraphrase:** The paper names two distinct uses of a confidence score — calibration (does 0.9 mean correct 90 per cent of the time?) and failure prediction (does higher confidence rank correct answers above wrong ones?) — but defines and reports only ECE in the main text; AUROC appears solely in the appendix tables. The two properties come apart there: on MathVista o3 records the lowest ECE in the column, 0.242, yet an AUROC of 0.586 that GPT-4.1 (0.632) and Qwen2-VL 72B (0.648) both beat, and Qwen2-VL 72B on MathVision records ECE 0.661 with AUROC 0.497, which is chance (page_index 39). For a pipeline that would use confidence to rank or threshold detections, discrimination is the relevant property and ECE alone does not report it.

## Optional framing hook (not counted in the salience cap)

- **Quote (verbatim):** "These findings collectively suggest that while verbalized uncertainty is promising for interpretable confidence estimation, its reliability in VLMs is highly sensitive to both modality and model design"
- **Locator:** page_index 6 · p.7
