## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Tian and colleagues ask whether reasoning is "truly a consistent performance booster for vision-language models" and answer no. The ICLR 2026 paper has two halves: a diagnosis of what longer reasoning does to perception, and a training remedy — VISION-ANCHORED POLICY OPTIMIZATION (VAPO), a reinforcement-learning replacement for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) — that we cannot use.

The probe is an "early decision" mode: three open 7B reasoning VLMs (R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinker, Vision-R1) are halted at logical breakpoints and made to answer immediately, tracking accuracy across 0–100% of the trace. Early reasoning helps substantially; gains then plateau and reverse, with MMStar and HallusionBench falling over 2% from their peak. The failure budget: GPT-5 categorisation of Vision-R1's errors puts perception at 55.23%, against 33.05% logical and 11.72% knowledge, with the harm concentrated on vision-intensive benchmarks, not geometry-style tasks with simple visual structure. A 32.35% recoverable ratio is claimed for those perception errors via early decision (page_index 4), but the caption calls it 'a loose metric based on a series of early decisions' (page_index 3), §B.16 'oracle-style'; single-cut ratios are 4.5–8.8%, aggregating to 15.4% (Table 19, page_index 28). The mechanism offered is visual forgetting: visual attention decays across generation steps to negligible levels, so decisions ride on the model's prior thoughts, not the image.

Three things bear on our design. Thinking mode: our task is point-symbol perception on scanned maps — vision-intensive by their taxonomy — so it is a strong published reason to treat a raised thinking budget as something to measure, not assume. Verifier placement: their anchor probe shows the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B's accuracy on simple visual claims decaying towards chance (around 0.5) late in the trace, cautioning against asking a model to adjudicate perceptual facts at the end of a long trace. Verifier independence: models trained on question-aligned claims scored worse than on question-independent ones (50.6 against 52.5 average benchmark accuracy over six benchmarks, 5,000-example subset; Table 7, page_index 22), which the authors attribute, hedged ('likely due to'), to the model settling such claims from its own reasoning, not the image (page_index 23) — the hazard our adversarial verifier faces if it inherits the proposer's rationale.

Three scope limits travel with all of it. Every model probed is an open 7B GRPO-family model; GPT-5-Thinking and Gemini-2.5-Pro appear only as OpenCompass leaderboard reference rows (page_index 7; §A.2, page_index 16), never analysed for visual forgetting, so extending the finding to Gemini's thinking budgets is our inference — though the authors expect visual forgetting to 'likely generalize' across architectures (§B.13, page_index 26). Every benchmark is question-answering, with no detection or localisation task, so the transfer to symbol detection is an analogy, not a result. And 'prolonged reasoning' here is short: average traces run 201–341 tokens per benchmark (Table 26, page_index 31), the attention analysis truncates output to 250 tokens as this 'basically covers the full response' (§A.4, page_index 18), and over 300 tokens counts as 'long-thought' (§B.21, page_index 30) — so a Gemini high-thinking trace sits well outside the measured range.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Open-vocabulary / VLM detection alternative is the primary cluster — this is a VLM-side result about the reasoning-versus-perception trade-off rather than a detector we could adopt — with consensus / test-time-compute antecedent as a close secondary, since its target is exactly the assumption that more test-time reasoning monotonically helps. For us it is the evidentiary basis for treating thinking mode as an empirical question rather than a dial to turn up: it predicts that on a vision-intensive, perception-bound task like point-symbol detection the returns to longer reasoning plateau and reverse, and it supplies a mechanism — visual attention decaying to negligible levels — for why. Its limits are equally load-bearing: the evidence comes from open 7B models fine-tuned with GRPO-family reinforcement learning on question-answering benchmarks, so it licenses a hypothesis about Gemini thinking budgets and a measurement, not a conclusion.

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "under vanilla reasoning, the attention of visual tokens exhibits a marked decline as reasoning progresses, eventually reaching negligible levels. This pattern indicates the model's decision-making becomes increasingly driven by its own historical thoughts rather than the visual cues, a phenomenon termed visual forgetting."
- **Locator:** page_index 4 · p.5 · §3.2 Side effect of reasoning: visual forgetting
- **Paraphrase:** Tracking, per generation step, the share of post-softmax attention that output tokens pay to image tokens (averaged over layers), the authors find it falls away as reasoning runs on, until the answer is effectively conditioned on the reasoning trace rather than on the image. They name this visual forgetting. The published curve is a single randomly selected MathVerse example from Vision-R1, with outputs truncated to the first 250 tokens for comparability (Fig. 3 caption, page_index 4; §A.4, page_index 18); the step from that example to 'under vanilla reasoning' generally is the authors' prose, not a reported across-model average. The evidence is an attention measurement on open-weight models, so it is unavailable to us on a closed API — the behavioural consequence is what transfers, not the diagnostic.
- **Relevance:** §5 discussion — why longer thinking might hurt symbol detection · Open-vocabulary / VLM detection alternative — mechanism behind reasoning-induced perception loss · **extends**

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "exhibits a clear downward trend in anchor scores as reasoning progresses, eventually approaching the level of random guessing (around 0.5)"
- **Locator:** page_index 21 · p.22 · §B.2 Visual anchor score (appendix)
- **Paraphrase:** Probed at the default K = 20 anchor positions along a reasoning trace with balanced true/false visual claims about the image, the base model before VAPO training (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, an instruction-tuned model rather than one of the three reasoning VLMs of §3.1) scores well early and decays towards chance (about 0.5) by the late positions; after VAPO training the same probe holds near 0.9 throughout. The decay is measured on the base model with a binary yes/no judgement, averaged over each benchmark's examples (MathVerse and MMStar shown).

### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "question-aligned claims are highly correlated with the question content, allowing the model to infer their correctness from its own reasoning traces without relying on visual input"
- **Locator:** page_index 23 · p.24 · §B.5 Effect of visual claims (appendix)
- **Paraphrase:** Claims written to match the example's question let the model adjudicate them from its own prior reasoning instead of from the image, which defeats the purpose of the probe: models trained with question-aligned claims average 50.6 benchmark accuracy over six benchmarks, against 52.5 for the default question-independent visual claims and 49.0 for vanilla GRPO, all on a 5,000-example training subset (Table 7, page_index 22). The paper's corresponding design rule is to generate claims from the image alone, withholding the question.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — verifier prompt construction · Adversarial verification — independence of the verifier from the proposer's rationale · **supports**

### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "this reinforcement of visual information alleviates the performance degradation observed during the reasoning process, with visual replay yielding particularly pronounced gains, ultimately outperforming vanilla reasoning by around 1.5%"
- **Locator:** page_index 5 · p.6 · §3.2 — 'Encouraging models to look more often boosts reasoning'
- **Paraphrase:** Two purely inference-time interventions — visual replay (re-inserting a downsampled copy of the image at intervals) and focus prompt (injecting instructions such as "I need to see the image") at four roughly evenly spaced points aligned to logical boundaries — both raise visual attention immediately and blunt the accuracy decline; visual replay gains about 1.5% over vanilla reasoning, averaged over the benchmarks and models tested. The authors' own caveats travel with this: the remedies incur substantial inference overhead and only partially alleviate visual forgetting, which is their stated motivation for a training-side fix.
- **Relevance:** §4 architecture — prompt design for a closed-API proposer · Open-vocabulary / VLM detection alternative — prompt-level mitigations available without retraining · **extends**
