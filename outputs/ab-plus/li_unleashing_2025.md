## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Li and colleagues ask the question our reasoning-mode conditions raise, though by a different route: does a model trained to think longer see better? On the benchmark they build for the purpose the answer is largely no. The comparison is between models — base LVLMs against separately RL-tuned reasoning models — not one model run at different thinking budgets; the paper reports no budget sweep, and the thinking configuration of the proprietary systems in Table 1 is unstated. DisTANCE is austere: 300 image-question pairs, 100 each for length, perimeter and area, over Python-synthesised images of 3 to 7 coloured shapes, scored by relative accuracy — RAavg averages a below-threshold-error indicator over {0.5, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1}, and RA0.1 is the strict high-precision figure. Every question is posed as a ratio (the image's height in radii of the red circle, say), so resolution is factored out by construction.

Two findings matter to us. First, reasoning-tuned LVLMs make only marginal and mixed gains over the bases they were tuned from — Vision-R1 at 22.7% and R1-OneVision at 21.1% average RAavg against Qwen2.5-VL-7B's 21.5% — and the frontier proprietary systems, though clearly ahead of the open models, remain poor in absolute terms: Gemini-2.5-Flash, our own model family, is the strongest system in the table at 23.3% RA0.1 and 51.9% RAavg, with area estimation at 14.0/34.0, and the authors still gloss this as reaching “only 51.9%”. Second, they offer a mechanism. Reasoning-tuned models emit longer answers with a smaller perception share: Table 2 puts Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 199 tokens and a 17.6% perception ratio against R1-OneVision-7B at 322 tokens and 12.4%, so the extra tokens go to reasoning, not to looking. They name the default behaviour Fast Perception — perception emitted as a one-shot judgement rather than a process — and show relative error on length estimation rising consistently with ground-truth distance (measured on the untuned base), inferring that step-by-step decomposition does not transfer to perceiving.

The remedy is training-side, and therefore not ours to take. Perception-Time Scaling makes perception verbose and stepwise, taught by supervised cold start on 6,000 synthesised chains, then GRPO under an exponential relative-error reward; Qwen2.5-VL-7B goes from 8.0% to 64.7% RA0.1 and 21.5% to 88.3% RAavg. The prompt- and tool-level family we could actually adopt does far less: against GPT-4o's 15.3/35.7 base, a partially reproduced DetToolChain gives 15.3/42.0 — flat at the strict threshold, up at the looser ones — and Visual Sketchpad 19.0/30.1, trading a small high-precision gain for a loss at looser thresholds; both are Table 3 rows on gpt-4o-0806 compared against a Table 1 GPT-4o whose build is not stated.

The transferable claim is a caution by analogy, not a recipe: what longer-chain training bought was reasoning tokens, not perception, and a thinking budget may spend its tokens the same way. The evidence base is narrow — synthetic 2D geometry, continuous magnitude estimation, 3B/7B backbones for the training work — but the direction is one our reasoning-mode comparison should expect to reproduce rather than contradict.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

An open-vocabulary/VLM-detection-alternative source used against the grain: not a detection paper, but direct evidence that inference-time scaling — kin to the mechanism behind our thinking-budget conditions, and loosely behind test-time compute generally — improves multimodal reasoning without improving perception, which is most of what symbol-spotting on a map actually is. It sits secondarily in the consensus/test-time-compute antecedent cluster as a boundary condition on what buying more inference-time compute can be expected to deliver — with the caveat that the paper's “inference-time scaling” means longer single-pass reasoning chains instilled by RLVR training, not repeated sampling with aggregation, so it bears on thinking budgets and not on consensus over passes: the paper's own fix is a training intervention (supervised cold start plus GRPO) that a prompted hosted model cannot adopt, and the prompt- and tool-level baselines it reports move GPT-4o's DisTANCE scores only marginally and in mixed directions. Take it as a calibration on expectations, not as prior art: the evidence is synthetic 2D geometry and continuous magnitude estimation — Table 1 evaluates four proprietary systems, eight open models from 3B to 78B and five 7B reasoning models, while the training work uses 3B/7B backbones — not symbol localisation on scanned maps.
