## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Davis, Lipo and Sanger compare four object-based image analysis (OBIA) methods for detecting earthen mounds and shell rings in LiDAR-derived digital elevation models at 1.2 m resolution across three study areas in Beaufort County, South Carolina: multiresolution segmentation, an inverted stochastic depression analysis (IDA), template matching (TM), and a combined segmentation-plus-TM algorithm. The headline is an ensemble finding — no single algorithm is consistently superior, the combination is most effective — which makes this the nearest disciplinary antecedent we have for combining complementary detectors rather than tuning a single one.

Three of its moves map onto our architecture. Template matching is a two-stage pipeline in which a permissive proposer is pruned by a discriminative second stage: 20 negative templates built from 393 non-archaeological topographic features, a rejection rule at 75 % likelihood of false positive, a 60 % "most statistically likely" retention floor, and land-use, waterbody and roadway exclusions take TM from over 10,000 raw identifications down to 10 candidates (§3.3, page_index 18); the combined method reuses the same 75 % negative-correlation rejection inside its own cascade (§3.4, page_index 18–19). That is an adversarial verifier in all but name, and it carries the expected recall cost — TM missed the shell ring that segmentation and IDA (at 300 iterations) both found, and the combined method missed a previously unrecorded mound that all three single methods located. Second, IDA exposes an explicit inference-time compute knob: 100, 200 and 300 Monte Carlo iterations, with reported accuracy rising 35.71 % → 50.00 % → 71.43 % while 100 iterations already cost 36 hours. Third, the class definition is hand-set and region-bound — Table 1's area, circularity, asymmetry and compactness thresholds, derived from a summary of known local features — which the authors themselves call contingency-bound and incapable of finding morphologies absent from the reference sample. That is the labour a prompted VLM does not require.

The cautionary half is the evidence base, and it is the part to carry into our related-work paragraph. Every accuracy figure in Tables 3, 5 and 6 rests on 22 ground-visited locations, entered in Table 3 as 12 surveyed detections for segmentation, 14 for IDA at 100 iterations, 6 for TM and 4 for the combined method, against candidate pools of 2,490, 5,422, 10 and 10. The winning method's perfect record is four out of four. Those percentages are precision on the surveyed handful; no recall rate is ever computed — the closest the paper comes is counting how many previously recorded Pinckney Island deposits each method recovered (Table 4, page_index 23; Supplemental Table 1, page_index 54–56), where TM and the combined method recovered none; and Table 6 averages two differently defined accuracies into an "overall utility" that then ranks the methods. The manual-versus-automated comparison the discussion offers was added at a reviewer's request (response to reviewers, page_index 2), not designed in — useful provenance for how we frame our own novice-human baseline.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Archaeological prospection prior art, and the primary cluster: the same object class (mounds, plus shell rings) detected automatically, but in LiDAR elevation data as area-extent morphology rather than as point symbols on a map sheet, which places it at the area-segmentation end of our difficulty ladder. Its unusual value is that it is a within-study comparison rather than a single-method demonstration, so it supplies archaeological precedent for three of our design choices at once — an ensemble beating any single detector on the surveyed sample (though that same combined method identified no features at all in Study Area 2 and recovered none of the previously recorded deposits there: page_index 21, 54), a negative-template rejection stage functioning as an adversarial verifier, and an explicit compute-for-accuracy knob at inference time — while its field-verification denominators (4 to 14 surveyed detections per method, against pools of 10 to 5,422 candidates) make it the cautionary case for how prospection comparisons get reported. - **Locator:** page_index 18 · p.19 · §3.3 Template Matching (accepted manuscript)
