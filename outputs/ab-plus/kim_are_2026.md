## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Donghwan Kim's preprint (arXiv:2607.20768v1, 22 July 2026) asks a measurement rather than a prescriptive question: do the diversity statistics used to choose which models to combine track diversity, or mostly re-express capability? Realised majority-vote gain (the vote's accuracy minus the best member's) is the dependent variable across all 31,900 size-2–4 subsets of 30 large language models on a 356-item MMLU-Pro slice, reproduced with 29 models and 338 items on TruthfulQA; five diversity-related measures are audited under six linear capability controls plus nonlinear and matched estimators.

Three results carry over. First, latent complementarity is not the bottleneck: oracle gain is positive in 100 per cent of subsets, yet under the conservative correct-count rule with in-sample best selection a size-3 vote beats its strongest member in only 9.98 per cent of them. The aggregation rule, not the pool, is where the signal is lost — but his oracle is 'not a real aggregator', only an upper bound; he audits 'that classical intuition rather than learned aggregators, routers, or judges'; and the one judge-shaped datum he cites runs the other way: in closed-loop table recognition, 'iteration produces better candidates that a reference-free LLM judge largely fails to select'. He motivates our adversarial verifier pass; he does not evidence it. Second, capability entanglement is severe: strict diversity is near-collinear with one minus mean member accuracy (ρ = +0.991 MMLU-Pro, +0.988 TruthfulQA), leaving only 1.1 and 1.5 per cent of its rank variance after best+mean control, and the three linearly coupled statistics (strict, disagreement, double-fault) surrender 84–99 per cent of theirs to ranked best and mean accuracy — descriptive in-sample projections, not held-out R² — Jaccard and focal diversity much less, so Kim declines to call all five interchangeable capability proxies. Third, after capability control the only directionally stable remainder is pairwise co-failure (more shared error, less gain), modest and configuration-dependent (roughly −0.18 to −0.57; held-out gain>0 AUC 0.597), 'a diagnosis of shortfall, not a recipe for winning ensembles'.

Three scoping caveats travel with any citation. Kim's pools are heterogeneous and multi-model, and he separates that himself from single-model self-sampling against an agent's own baseline — our architecture — so his beat-the-best-member bar constrains our thinking-mode-mixture claim, not our consensus-over-passes claim. All of it is correlational and exploratory on heavily overlapping subsets reusing 30 models, so no subset-level p-values are reported; the evidence is direction, effect size, and model-level resampling stability. And the audit runs under one zero-shot chain-of-thought regime, a boundary condition Kim flags himself.

Its most portable gift is metric hygiene. The 1.27 per cent pooled gain>0 rate mixes structural size-2 zeros and size-4 tie effects with that informative size-3 rate, and Kim reports that split at every level: the abstract leads with the size-3 rate and calls the pooled one 'partly reflecting deterministic even-size voting behavior', with §5.1, Figure C4 and Limitations item 4 each decomposing it. What transfers is his stratified reporting, not an instance of the aggregate-across-strata trap. His held-out best-member analysis is our carried-versus-oracle distinction under another name.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Consensus / test-time-compute antecedent, and the most deflationary source in that cluster: it audits whether the statistics that would license a 'diversity dividend' reading measure anything separable from member capability, and finds that the three linearly coupled contingency-table statistics largely do not. Its object differs from ours in a way that must be stated wherever it is cited — heterogeneous multi-model pools voting on multiple-choice benchmarks, judged against the stringent bar of beating the strongest available member, where our consensus is repeated sampling of one proposer against a single-pass baseline — so it constrains our reasoning-mode-mixture claim rather than our consensus-over-passes claim. Read as method rather than as result it supplies two transferable disciplines: control for capability level and spread before crediting diversity, and validate any operating point chosen on the evaluation set against a held-out selection.

- **Quote (verbatim):** "latent complementarity is always present, yet simple voting converts it into a win in only 9.98% of all canonical size-3 subsets."
- **Locator:** page_index 5 · p.6 · §5.1 Majority voting rarely beats the best member
- **Paraphrase:** Across both benchmarks every subset contains latent complementarity — a hypothetical oracle aggregator that is correct whenever any member is correct gains in 100% of subsets — yet a plain majority vote converts that into an actual win over the strongest single member in fewer than one size-3 subset in ten. ### KP2
- **Quote (verbatim):** "our joint-correctness proxy ("strict diversity") is nearly collinear with one minus mean accuracy (size-3 Spearman ρ = +0.991 / +0.988), helping explain why several counter-intuitive raw diversity–gain associations attenuate or become specification-dependent under capability control."
- **Locator:** page_index 1 · p.2 · §1 Introduction — contribution 2, entanglement with capability
- **Paraphrase:** The paper's joint-correctness proxy, 'strict diversity' (the complement of the rate at which both members are correct), is almost perfectly rank-correlated with one minus mean member accuracy — ρ = +0.991 on MMLU-Pro and +0.988 on TruthfulQA at ensemble size 3 — so a statistic presented as diversity is largely a restatement of how capable the members are. Kim offers that collinearity as 'helping explain why' several raw diversity–gain associations attenuate, reverse, or turn specification-dependent once capability is controlled. The paper's own limit travels with the point: the results should not be read as evidence that error diversity is unimportant for aggregation, only that these observational statistics do not isolate error structure from capability, and Jaccard and focal diversity retain substantially more residual variation than the three coupled statistics do.
- **Relevance:** §5 discussion — what a thinking-mode diversity dividend can and cannot be attributed to · Consensus / test-time-compute antecedent — the capability confound in diversity claims · **complicates**

### KP3
- **Quote (verbatim):** "after capability adjustment, this pairwise co-failure direction is the only contingency-table component that remains directionally stable, while several commonly interpreted diversity signals do not provide separable information."
- **Locator:** page_index 8 · p.9 · §6 Discussion
- **Paraphrase:** After controlling for best and mean member accuracy, the only contingency-table component whose direction stays stable is pairwise co-failure: the more often two members are wrong on the same item, the smaller the vote's gain. Kim treats the sign itself as unsurprising — shared errors directly reduce the cases a vote can recover — and locates the contribution in the measurement diagnosis that the other commonly interpreted signals are not separable. The weakening caveats travel: magnitude is configuration-dependent, with point estimates spanning approximately −0.18 to −0.57 across slice, roster, benchmark, and threshold configurations, the size-4 MMLU-Pro model-resampling interval crossing zero, the association near zero in a restricted nine-model Chinese-origin pool, and held-out prediction of the rare gain>0 events reaching only AUC 0.597 — 'a diagnosis of shortfall, not a recipe for winning ensembles'.

### KP4
- **Quote (verbatim):** "but they largely concern single-model self-sampling gains over an agent's own baseline, not when voting over a heterogeneous pool beats the strongest available member."
- **Locator:** page_index 1 · p.2 · §2.2 Voting and self-consistency in LLMs
- **Paraphrase:** Kim separates his object of study from the self-consistency and multi-agent literature: those results largely concern one model sampling itself repeatedly and beating its own single-sample baseline, not a heterogeneous pool of different models beating the strongest member available. Our consensus-over-passes is the first kind, so the 9.98% headline is not a prediction about our design; it is the bar Kim sets for the second kind, which is the closer analogue of a mixed thinking-mode pool. One further scope condition sharpens the distinction: the audit decodes at temperature 0.0 with provider defaults elsewhere (Appendix Table A3), so every unit of 'diversity' it measures comes from model heterogeneity, none from stochastic sampling of one model — which is exactly where our pass-to-pass variation comes from.

### KP5
- **Quote (verbatim):** "The 1.27% all-subset rate mixes structural size-2 zeros and size-4 tie effects with the more informative size-3 rate (9.98%)."
- **Locator:** page_index 9 · p.10 · §8 Limitations — item 4, voting-rule and baseline conditionality
- **Paraphrase:** The pooled 1.27% positive-gain rate across sizes 2–4 is not one small effect but a mixture of structurally different strata: under the correct-count rule a size-2 majority vote can never exceed its best member (identically 0%), size 4 is 0% largely because exact 2–2 ties are scored incorrect, and only size 3, at 9.98%, carries interpretable information. Kim states the decomposition at every level of the paper: the abstract qualifies the pooled rate as 'partly reflecting deterministic even-size voting behavior' and leads with 9.98% instead, §5.1 gives the full split inline, Figure C4 decomposes the gain>0 rate by ensemble size and rule, and Limitations item 4 restates it. This is the aggregate-across-heterogeneous-strata problem in ensemble clothing, and the transferable lesson is the stratified reporting rather than a failure of it.
- **Relevance:** §3 methods — reporting conventions for pooled rates · Metric hygiene — aggregate versus stratified reporting · **supports**

### KP6
- **Quote (verbatim):** "in-sample best 1.27 9.98 0.00 held-out best 3.44 (±1.18) 18.71 (±3.70) 1.23 (±0.95)"
- **Locator:** page_index 5 · p.6 · §5.1 — Table 2, in-sample versus held-out best-member selection
- **Paraphrase:** Table 2 gives the positive-gain rate under two baselines. With the strongest member identified on the same items used to score the vote, size-3 ensembles win 9.98% of the time; selecting that member on one random half of the 356 common items and scoring gain on the other, meaned over 20 seeds, raises it to 18.71% (±3.70), and the pooled size-2–4 rate from 1.27% to 3.44% (±1.18). Size 2 is omitted because positive gain is structurally impossible there. Nearly doubling the size-3 win rate by denying the baseline sight of the evaluation items shows how much of the apparent shortfall is an artefact of an in-sample-selected baseline — knowing which member is best is information a deployment does not have.
- **Relevance:** §6 deployment — transfer taxes and operating-point selection · Calibration transfer — carried versus oracle operating points · **extends**

### KP7
- **Quote (verbatim):** "All models were evaluated under a common zero-shot chain-of-thought instruction. Different prompting or reasoning regimes may alter both member capability and error dependence"
- **Locator:** page_index 9 · p.10 · §8 Limitations — item 6, prompt-regime and parsing conditionality
- **Paraphrase:** Every model in the audit was evaluated under a single zero-shot chain-of-thought instruction, and the author flags this as a boundary condition rather than a housekeeping detail: a different prompting or reasoning regime may change both how capable a member is and how its errors depend on other members'. He concludes that the measured relationships should not be assumed invariant to the prompt regime. Read against our design, it is a hedged external-validity caveat rather than a finding: Kim never varies the reasoning regime and reports nothing about mixing regimes within a pool, so what he offers is the warning that a reasoning-mode setting may not be a neutral diversity knob. Combined with his own recommendation to 'control for capability level and spread, including nonlinear specifications, before crediting diversity', it says a gain observed when mixing thinking modes should not be credited to diversity until capability is controlled.
- **Relevance:** §4 architecture — thinking-mode configuration and pass diversity · Consensus / test-time-compute antecedent — reasoning-mode settings as a design variable · **extends**
