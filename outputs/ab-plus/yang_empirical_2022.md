## Summary (Claude's synthesis — advisory, unverified)

Yang and colleagues at Microsoft present PICa, which converts an image into a caption or a tag list and prompts a frozen GPT-3 with a handful of in-context question–answer pairs, so that knowledge retrieval and reasoning happen in one query rather than in a retrieve-then-reason pipeline. On OK-VQA (14,055 image–question pairs, five ground-truth answers each, scored with VQAv2 soft accuracy) sixteen in-context examples reach 48.0% against a supervised state of the art of 39.4% trained on the entire training set. Published at AAAI-22 and claiming the first use of GPT-3 for multimodal tasks, it states four years before our study the annotation-budget argument we make: a general model, prompted, beating a task-trained one on a fraction of the task supervision — the saving is in VQA annotations; the captioner and tagger in front of the frozen GPT-3 are themselves supervised.

Three mechanisms prefigure ours. Multi-query ensemble is a repeated-querying antecedent — n×k examples are split across k prompts, GPT-3 is queried k times, and the answer with the highest summed log-probability wins. The arbiter is model-internal likelihood rather than agreement across passes, the branch our greedy consensus did not take, and the diversity source is disjoint exemplar subsets rather than decoding noise, since decoding is formulated as an argmax. Its yield shrinks as the prompt strengthens: at n=1, k=1 to k=5 moves 36.4 to 40.8; at n=16 only 46.5 to 48.0. In-context example selection by CLIP question and image similarity is the second, lifting random selection from 43.3 to 46.5 at n=16 — but deliberately dissimilar examples drop to 40.1, below random. Exemplar choice can hurt. The third is modality: a blind baseline with an empty image string still reaches 30.1 in the full configuration against 46.9 with VinVL COCO captions, so about 64 per cent of the caption-based score survives never seeing the image.

Two hygiene traps travel with the headline. “Few-shot” here is not an annotation budget of sixteen — Table 2's “Example engineering” column marks that PICa-Full needs a pool larger than n to select from, and the strictly few-shot configuration is PICa-Base at 43.3 with captions and tags. Several of the best numbers are also marked oracle: ground-truth captions (48.7 and 53.3) and answer-similarity selection (49.1). The authors themselves control for a third trap — the captioner behind 48.0 was fine-tuned on COCO 2014 train while OK-VQA test images come from COCO 2014 validation, and their stricter Conceptual Captions captioner scores 44.0 against 46.9 with captions alone.

Most consequentially for us, the win is benchmark-shaped. On perception-heavy VQAv2 the same method reaches 56.1% against supervised Oscar's 73.8%, which the authors call around 17% lower, and they name the reason: the image is abstracted as text, so colour and counting questions fail. Our task is perception, not knowledge — which is the condition under which this source's headline claim is weakest and its cautions most transferable.

## Positioning annotation (interpretive)

Few-shot / in-context learning and annotation budget is where this sits: an early instance of a frozen general model, given sixteen in-context examples and no parameter update, beating a model trained on the whole task corpus — the argument our study makes for prompted VLMs against trained detectors, stated four years earlier in a different modality, though the sixteen-example budget covers VQA supervision only and a fine-tuned captioner sits in front of the frozen model. It is also a consensus antecedent worth naming precisely: its multi-query ensemble is repeated querying with a likelihood arbiter rather than an agreement arbiter, and its gains shrink as the prompt improves, which is a prior our own pass-count results should be read against. Its two qualifications are the ones we must carry rather than quote around — “few-shot” here presumes an example pool larger than n, and the headline win is on a knowledge-heavy benchmark, whereas on perception-heavy VQAv2 the same method sits about 17 points under supervised Oscar, and our symbol-detection task is perception-heavy.

- **Paraphrase:** Repeated querying with a likelihood arbiter: n×k examples are distributed across k prompts, the model is queried k times, and the candidate with the greatest summed token log-probability is returned.
