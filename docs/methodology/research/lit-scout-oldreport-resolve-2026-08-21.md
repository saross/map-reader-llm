## Per-row verification notes

**Row 1 — Zhang et al. (2022), ISPRS IJGI 11(11):540.** Venue, year, and metrics confirmed. Precision **97.06%**, recall **99.72%**, mAP **99.50%** are verbatim from the registry abstract, so the old report's "P = 0.971, R = 0.997, mAP = 0.995" is accurate. **Two corrections.** (a) The domain is *not* "Chinese city maps": §3.1 states the data are "three different styles of Chinese provincial atlases", scanned, 6,675 labelled images, 12 POI-type symbol classes (school, hospital, market, government sector, bus station, mountain, post office, port, edifice, bank, PSB, hotel). (b) The paper never states an IoU threshold for mAP — "mAP@0.50" is a plausible inference from the YOLOv3/PASCAL VOC convention but is not in the text; drop the `@0.50` or mark it as inferred. The "Clean/rendered" difficulty label in the old table is also generous, since these are scanner outputs, though modern atlas symbology is far cleaner than 1:50k Soviet sheets.

**Row 2 — Duan et al. (2025), DIGMAPPER.** Eleven authors (Duan, Gerlek, Minton, Knoblock, F. Lin, T. Chen, Jang, Kirsanova, Z. Li, Y. Lin, Chiang); USC ISI / Minnesota / USGS attribution is right; ACM SIGSPATIAL 2025 industry track. **TOPAZ F1 = 0.98 is correct but needs qualifying**: Table 2 gives median F1 by map category — Excellent **0.98** (2 maps, median IoU 0.95), Good **0.77** (5 maps), Fair **0.28** (4 maps). The paper's own prose conflates categories ("achieving an F1 score of 0.98 and an Ex-gt ratio of 0.99" splices the Excellent F1 with the Good ex-gt ratio), so quote the table, not the sentence. **The significant correction is the point module.** §2.4 reports YOLOv8 trained on 10,000 synthetic patches (~3,000 per class, symbols harvested from USGS documentation via a GPT-based table-parsing step) plus human annotations from 100 USGS maps, evaluated on 10 maps / 5 symbol types / 2,404 instances with a match buffer of 2×10⁻⁴ × map-diagonal. Results: **overall P 0.83, R 0.81, F1 0.82**; by category Excellent **0.89** (3 maps), Good **0.56** (5), Fair **0.05** (2); by symbol, inclined bedding 0.91 down to lineation 0.13. A human-annotation-only ablation gives F1 0.79. This is the single most consequential finding of this sweep for D.1.

**Row 3 — Kirsanova et al. (2025), GeoSearch '25 @ ACM SIGSPATIAL '25, Minneapolis, pp. 35–38.** CrossRef author order is Kirsanova, Duan, Chiang; the arXiv record and the PDF running head give Kirsanova, Chiang, Duan — either way "Kirsanova et al." **Metrics confirmed exactly**: Table 2 gives GPT-4o (15 examples) legend item IoU 0.85 / **F1 0.88** and description IoU 0.88 / **F1 0.92**, against a LayoutLMv3 baseline of 0.75 / 0.79. The 15-example optimum is confirmed (Table 1: 5→10→15 improves, 20 degrades). **The task is legend item–description linking, as claimed — not symbol detection on the map face.** Pipeline: LARA crops the legend area, then GPT-4o predicts bounding boxes for item–description pairs; 40 annotated maps from the DARPA-USGS dataset; IoU 0.5 match threshold; GPT-4o April 2025, temperature 0, deterministic prompts. **One description correction**: "15 in-context visual examples" is wrong in kind. Each query carries *one* example legend image plus *one* target legend image, and the "15 examples" are 15 item–description coordinate pairs enumerated inside a ~1.2k-token JSON prompt drawn from that single example image. So the visual-exemplar count is one, not fifteen. For D.1 this is the paper you must engage with directly: it is a real VLM bounding-box detection result on historical map scans, so the absence claim needs narrowing to "no published work performs VLM detection of point symbols across the map face", and the "closest analogue uses visual exemplars" sentence should be restated as "one exemplar legend image plus fifteen structured coordinate examples".

> **Verifier note on row 3**: the description IoU in the note above should read **0.84**, not 0.88 — the 0.88 was carried across from the adjacent item-F1 cell. All other figures confirmed against `arXiv:2510.08385`.

**Row 4 — Sterzinger et al., ICDAR 2025 (LNCS, online 2025-09-17; print 2026); arXiv 2506.21826.** Three authors: Sterzinger, Peer, Sablatnig (TU Wien). **F1 = 0.827 is real but mis-labelled as 10-shot.** §4.4: "we achieve 77.1 for the former [IoU] and 82.7 for the latter [F1-Score] on the test set", alongside mean PQ 67.3% (2nd place in the ICDAR 2021 rankings). But §4.1 states the ICDAR 2021 dataset "consists of one training image, one validation image, and three test images" — cropped into non-overlapping 448×448 patches — so this is a single-training-image regime, not 10-shot. The 10-shot and 5-shot experiments are on the Siegfried vineyard/railway datasets (+5% and +13% relative mIoU at 10-shot, ~+20% at 5-shot). Method is RADIO-L with low-rank adaptation, 689k trainable parameters (0.21% of model size). The old report's use of this row to support Claim 4 ("even few-shot approaches require annotated examples") still stands, but the specific "10-shot: F1 = 0.827" pairing must be split.

**Row 5 — Huang et al. (2023), ISPRS IJGI 12(3):128.** Seven authors (W. Huang, Sun, Yu, Guo, Q. Xu, Wen, L. Xu). **Description accurate.** ASPP substituted for SPP inside YOLOv4 (CSPDarknet53 backbone, PANet neck, CIoU loss, DIoU-NMS, k-means++ anchors) — the "ASPP-YOLOv4" label is the paper's own. Datasets: 1,909 scanned and 2,505 vectorised map images at Chinese national fundamental scales (1:500 / 1:1000 / 1:2000 symbol specification), 10 symbol classes, 7,471 symbol instances in the vectorised subset. In-domain result mAP **98.11%**, mIoU 0.876. **The +16.33% is confirmed and is the interesting number**: training on vectorised maps and testing on scanned maps gives recall of only 48.05%; adding Gaussian-blur-plus-colour-jitter style transfer raises recall by **17.56%**, F1 by **16.33%**, and mAP by **3.69%** — and even then the cross-domain result is recall 65.61% / mAP 80.09%, which the authors themselves call inadequate. Note the report's framing "transfer learning" is the paper's own word, but the mechanism is style-transfer data augmentation, not weight transfer. This is your cleanest citation for "supervised point-symbol detectors are brittle across the render→scan domain gap".

**Row 6 — Huang et al. (2025), PEACE, CVPR 2025.** Eleven authors; Microsoft Research attribution confirmed (`github.com/microsoft/PEACE`, Furu Wei senior author). **Metrics confirmed verbatim in the abstract**: GeoMap-Agent **0.811** overall on GeoMap-Bench vs GPT-4o **0.369**. One caveat for D.1: GeoMap-Bench evaluates extraction, referring, grounding, reasoning, and analysis as a question-answering benchmark, not bounding-box detection — so cite it as evidence of general-VLM weakness on cartographic material, never as a detection baseline. Separately, the old report at line 41 attributes the 0.369 figure to "CartoMapQA (Ung et al., ACM SIGSPATIAL 2025)"; that is a splice — the 0.369 is PEACE's own number, and the CartoMapQA attribution was not part of this worklist and remains unverified.

**Row 7 — Shteriyanov et al. (2025), Results in Engineering.** Four authors (Shteriyanov, Dzhusupova, Bosch, Olsson; TU Eindhoven / Chalmers / Malmö). All four quoted phrases in the old report — "notable degradation in cluttered environments, confusion with visually similar symbols, and hallucination of symbols", "not yet suitable for autonomous deployment" — are faithful to the abstract and §4/§5. **50.5% needs qualifying in two ways.** (a) It is **Gemini 2.5 Pro alone**, the best of four; the full spread is Gemini 2.5 Pro 50.5% (R 74.48, P 53.16), Qwen 2.5 VL 72B 40.5%, GPT-4o 30%, InternVL 2.5 78B 4.5%. (b) The metric is a custom **Exact Match Rate** requiring correctness on both symbol counts and labels — far stricter than F1, so it is not comparable to your F1 numbers. Data are 500 **synthetic** P&IDs split into 16 regions of 860×860 px, five target symbol classes, one-shot visual in-context querying. An ablation with an alternate exemplar holds EMR at 50.5%, which the authors read as evidence the exemplar identity does not drive the result. For your paper the pointed detail is that the top performer is the same model family you use, and its failure modes (clutter, hallucination, precision far below recall) mirror the false-positive behaviour documented in your own image-track experiments.

**Bonus correction, not on the worklist.** Row 10 of the old report's benchmark table attributes 10.1016/j.ecoinf.2022.101557 to "Mårtensson et al. (Ecological Informatics) 2022". ## Fit judgements for D.1 positioning

**Direct comparators (must appear in D.1).** Row 2, DIGMAPPER, is now the load-bearing one: it is the only verified point-symbol detection result on degraded historical maps with a stated match tolerance, and at F1 0.82 overall / 0.89 on clean sheets it sits close enough to your 0.796–0.885 range that the comparison has to be made carefully rather than asserted. Its tolerance (2×10⁻⁴ × map diagonal, "approximately the point symbol size") is a different protocol from your 50 m ground-distance threshold, and its "Excellent" tier is digitally-born or high-resolution scans, which is not your domain — both are legitimate grounds for arguing the tasks are not equivalent, but the F1 = 0.73 / "23-point improvement" framing cannot survive as written. Row 3, Kirsanova et al., is the comparator for the novelty claim rather than the performance claim, and it needs an explicit sentence distinguishing legend-region parsing from map-face detection.

**Contrast class (supervision-cost axis).** Rows 1 and 5 anchor the "what full supervision buys you, and where it breaks" pole: Zhang et al. reach mAP 99.5% on standardised atlas symbology with 6,675 labelled images, and Huang et al. reach mAP 98.11% in-domain but collapse to 48.05% recall across a render→scan shift that is much milder than yours. Row 4, Sterzinger et al., is the low-annotation contrast: the strongest historical-map result obtainable with parameter-efficient tuning, but for areal and linear features, which reinforces the polygon/point asymmetry the old report identified.

**VLM-limitations context.** Rows 6 and 7 together supply the "prevailing expectation" your result argues against — PEACE for cartographic material specifically (GPT-4o 0.369), BlueprintSymVL for technical symbology under one-shot querying (best model 50.5% EMR, with hallucination and clutter as named failure modes). Both are best used to frame Claim 5, and both need the caveat the old report already half-articulates: they test general-purpose application without task-specific prompt engineering, which is exactly the variable your study manipulates.
