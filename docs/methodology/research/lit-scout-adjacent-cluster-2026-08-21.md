## Per-row verification notes

**Row 1 — MEP Siamese-DINO. Report description fully correct.** Verified from the open-access author manuscript (Cambridge Apollo repository, `mep.txt` in scratchpad): "For the 13 object types (192 instances) included in training, it achieved a precision of 88.6%, recall of 89.5%, and F1 score of 0.89. For the 5 unseen object types (99 instances) … precision of 98.9%, recall of 90.9%, and F1 score of 0.95." The DINO attribution is correct and specific: "The Siamese network architecture is based on Facebook Research's official DINO (self-distillation with no labels)", using "a pre-trained ResNet50 model from DINO as the feature extractor". Query-based few-shot confirmed (query objects = EC samples). *Framing caveat for the comparison table*: this is self-supervised foundation-model features, not "traditional CV" — the classification in the old report's column is misleading. Seven authors → "et al." correct. Journal of Building Engineering, vol. 115.

**Row 2 — ArchNetv2. Description correct; the paper title differs from the label used.** The journal abstract gives "a convolutional neural network (CNN) based architecture, ArchNetv2 … includes a convolutional block attention module … mAP of 93.5%". The abstract does *not* name YOLOv8, so it was chased: the lead author's University of Alberta thesis states "ArchNetv2 is designed and developed based on the YOLOv8 for detecting objects in CAT floor plans", and describes an AC-CBAM (aggregated C2f CBAM) module replacing C2f, plus a fourth detection head. So **YOLOv8 + CBAM is verified**, just not from the paper abstract alone. Two minor glosses to correct: the paper's own term is "complex architectural floor plans" (raster images), not "CAD architectural plans"; and mAP is 93.5% exactly, so "mAP > 0.93" is safe. Predecessor ArchNet (v1) was YOLOv3-based at mAP 72% — useful if a trajectory point is wanted.

**Row 3 — GMFS. Journal attribution is wrong; metric is doubtful.** Correct venue is **Journal of Infrastructure Intelligence and Resilience** (KeAi/Elsevier, ISSN 2772-9915), *not* Developments in the Built Environment. Year is ambiguous by design: DOI stem and OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar say 2024 (online-first), CrossRef `issued` is 2025-06 (print issue), so `metadata` returns 2025 — 2025 is used in the claims block for verifier consistency, but cite as 2024 if the online-first convention is preferred. The acronym is confirmed from the abstract: "GPT-integrated Multi-object Few-shot SAM (GMFS)", segmenting "rooms and doors using only five reference samples", masks then classified by GPT-4, validated on CubiCasa and Rent3D. **The F1 = 0.86 claim could not be verified** — the abstract carries no numbers, the article is gold OA but only behind ScienceDirect (HTTP 403, no mirror in OpenAlex/Unpaywall). A ScienceDirect-derived search rendering reports "average precision of 0.86 and recall of 0.87, leading to an average F1 score of 0.87", which suggests the old report took the precision figure and labelled it F1. Treat as **unresolved pending full text** — institutional access settles it in a minute. Also note the paper's actual framing is accessibility-feature extraction (egress, wheelchair clear space), not generic room/door parsing. Two authors → "Zhang & Zhang", *not* "et al.".

**Row 4 — DEViT. Two corrections.** The paper is titled "**ArrowFormer: detecting painted symbols using DEViT**" — DEViT is the DETR+ViT model, ArrowFormer the system. Venue is right (SPIE, Eighteenth International Conference on Machine Vision, ICMV 2025, Paris, 19–22 October 2025) but the **proceedings volume 14114 was published 2026-02-25**, so `metadata` returns year 2026; "SPIE ICMV 2025 (2025)" will not match a mechanical check. Authors are Țarcă, Comănac, Coroamă, and Măcinic (note the Romanian diacritics — Ț and ă will need proper encoding in BibTeX). **The macro F1 = 0.930 figure is indicative only**: SPIE's page is JavaScript-rendered, CrossRef holds no abstract, and no mirror carries it. A web rendering of the SPIE abstract reports macro F1 92.96% on CeyMo against a 90.18% prior best — consistent with 0.930, but not mechanically grounded. Flag as needing the PDF if it is ever load-bearing. Zero citations, brand new, weakest item in the batch.

Metric confirmed verbatim.** arXiv abstract: "Grounding DINO achieves a 52.5 AP on the COCO detection zero-shot transfer benchmark, i.e., without any training data from COCO." The nuance matters for framing: it is **zero-shot transfer** AP, not supervised COCO AP. Published as an ECCV 2024 chapter (LNCS, *Computer Vision – ECCV 2024*, pp. 38–55), online 2024-11-23 with a **2025 print-volume date** — hence `metadata` returns 2025 while the paper is universally cited as ECCV 2024.
