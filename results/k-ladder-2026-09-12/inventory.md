**What this is.** For every proposer family in the register, which pass-count
(K) rungs exist as committed VERIFIED cells whose non-K parameters are
identical, and what each gap would cost to fill. Generated by
`scripts/build_k_ladder_inventory.py`; the machine-readable form is
`inventory.json` and the full per-family tables are `inventory-tables.md`.
Every figure below is read from those two generated files or from the file
cited beside it.

## 1. What R1 fixes, and how it is matched

Ruling R1 fixes the verifier at every rung: "the carried Gemini 3 verifier,
`verify_adversarial-text`, T = 0.0, n = 1, MINIMAL; no verifier swaps"
(`planning/k-ladder-review-2026-09-11.md` § 4). The register spells that
configuration out as four fields — instruction file `verify_adversarial.md`,
model `gemini-3-flash-preview`, thinking level `minimal`, temperature 0.0 —
and the phrase `verify_adversarial-text` is the project's shorthand for it, not
a filename: `prompts/system-instructions/` holds `verify_adversarial.md`,
`verify_adversarial_v2.md`, `verify_brief.md`, `verify_checklist.md` and
`verify_comparative.md`, and no `verify_adversarial-text.md`. The same
shorthand appears verbatim in the `_note` of each `stride-phaseb-2026-08-25`
ladder row ("Carry-forward verifier (verify_adversarial-text, T=0.0, MINIMAL,
n=1)", `results/run-conditions.json`). The inventory therefore matches on the
recorded fields.

**A family is a ladder only within one evaluation recipe.** The frame and the
reference are part of the family key, not a property of it: a 55-map pool
scored against the r2, canonical and standardised references is three ladders,
not one ladder with three recipes, and mixing them would compare cells across
strata. This is why the counts below are larger than a per-pool count would be.

## 3. The expectation in the controlling card did not hold

The card expected (§ 3 step 1) "the Gemini 3 MINIMAL text family is complete or
nearly so; 3.7 has K = 5 and 10 only; image has K = 5 and 10 only". The second
and third are right. The first is **wrong**, and the reason matters:

- Every Gemini 3 `pv-diag-384` family — MINIMAL text, HIGH text, MINIMAL image,
  HIGH image, and the scale-4 pool — carries exactly **two** rungs, K = 5 and
  K = 10, at each of T 0.3, T 0.7 and T 1.0. Twelve such families, two rungs
  each.
- The `pv-diag-384` K = 1 and K = 3 conditions the card counted (24 and 6) are
  counts over **all 128 conditions of that run**, including consensus-only and
  single-pass-baseline rows. Filtered to `aggregation == "verified"` and grouped
  by pool, the K = 3 verified cells are two off-board `-opmax` rows at T = 0.0
  (`pv-diag-384::pv-min-text-t0.0-n3-opmax`,
  `pv-diag-384::pv-high-text-t0.0-n3-opmax`) plus
  `n1-outstanding-384::pv-n1-image-t0-n3-opmax`; and T = 0.0 is a **different
  family** from T 0.3 / 0.7 / 1.0, whose pools hold only three passes on disk
  (`outputs/h11/pv-diag-384/flash-high-text-n5/text-t0.0/run_1..run_3`). So
  T = 0.0 is a one-rung family, not the K = 3 rung of a longer ladder.
- The twelve K = 1 `verified-adv-*-baseline` rows of `pv-diag-384` and the eight
  of `proposer-verifier-384` are single-pass proposers with their own pools
  (`text-baseline`, `image-baseline`, `pro-medium-text-baseline`,
  `pro-medium-image-baseline`, `detect_brief-text`) — they are not pass 1 of the
  `flash-*-n5` pools, so they are not the K = 1 rung of any of these ladders.
  They matter for step 4 (board admission under R3), not for step 1.

## 4. The gaps, and what they would cost

Over the 24 multi-rung families, `inventory.json` classes the 96 (family, K)
cells as: **61 committed**, **30 needs-verifier-pass**, **3
zero-usd-inherited**, **2 no-passes**. Zero gaps are `zero-usd-exact`.

**No gap is fillable at US$0 exactly, and the reason is measured, not asserted.**
A verifier's `probabilities.json` is keyed `candidate_{i:05d}` to feature *i* of
the union the verifier saw, so an existing verifier output can serve a shorter
first-N rung only if the rung's candidate set is a position-matched subset of
that union. `scripts/probe_subpool_verifier_coverage.py` tested that on six
committed sub-pool / longer-union pairs
(`subpool-coverage-probe.json`, tolerance 0.2 m, coordinates reprojected to
EPSG:32635 because the committed consensus files carry WGS84 degrees with no
`crs` member):

| probe | sub-pool n | longer n | positions agreeing | sub-pool points present anywhere in the longer union | prefix |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| MINIMAL text T0.3, 5-pass vs 10-pass | 1,388 | 1,575 | 128 | 312 | no |
| MINIMAL text T0.7, 5-pass vs 10-pass | 1,593 | 1,953 | 107 | 401 | no |
| MINIMAL text T0.7, 10-pass vs 30-pass | 1,953 | 2,790 | 113 | 366 | no |
| HIGH text T0.3, 5-pass vs 10-pass | 2,954 | 4,313 | 341 | 1,340 | no |
| MINIMAL image T0.7, 5-pass vs 10-pass | 1,123 | 1,450 | 82 | 300 | no |
| HIGH image T1.0, 5-pass vs 10-pass | 2,840 | 4,638 | 412 | 1,805 | no |

**0 of 6 covered.** The mechanism is visible in the second figure: not only is
the order different, most of the sub-pool's centroids do not exist in the longer
union *at all* within 0.2 m. `merge_passes.cluster_across_passes` records each
cluster's **mean** centroid over its members
(`scripts/merge_passes.py:289-292`), so adding passes 6–10 moves the centroid of
every cluster that gains a member. A 5-pass union is therefore a different
candidate universe from a 10-pass union, not a subset of it — which is the same
conclusion `planning/uplift-supplement-2026-08-28.md` reached for the 55-map
rungs ("clustering over 3 passes is not clustering over 10 restricted to 3"),
now measured on the GS pools as well.

Two consequences:

1. **Every K = 1 and K = 3 gap in the twelve Gemini 3 `pv-diag-384` families,
   and in the 3.7 GS screens, is `needs-verifier-pass`** — Phase 2, costed in
   `reports/k-ladder-phase2-costing-2026-09-12.md`, not run here.
2. **Probability inheritance is a different instrument, and it is already in
   the register.** The 55-map and 3.7-arm ladders were built by
   `cluster_first_n` plus nearest-neighbour inheritance within 10 m
   (`scripts/stride55_ladder.py`, docstring and `INHERIT_TOL_M`), a method its
   own header records as validated at ±0.008 on the gold standard. That reaches
   a missing rung at US$0 but as an approximation. Three gaps are classed
   `zero-usd-inherited` on that basis: the K = 5 rung of the Stride-B 3.7-verifier
   ladder, and the K = 1 and K = 3 rungs of the Stride-B canonical-reference
   group (three cells whose committed evaluations record no `cli_args.bounds`,
   so the inventory cannot read their frame; they are the 55-map corrected-F1
   engine's own output format, not `evaluate_detections.py`'s). The two
   `no-passes` gaps are the 3.7 arms' K = 10 rung: those arms hold five
   proposer passes, so the rung cannot be built at all.

## 5. What step 5 can score at US$0

The eight families of § 2, unchanged, with no new verifier call. Six are R1
ladders; two swap the verifier and are reported separately. One is on the gold
standard (`grid_common_bounds`, curator reference) and seven are on the 55-map
corpus at 50 m.

**Read each ladder at its own headline buffer.** The tables in
`inventory-tables.md` carry F1 at both 20 m and 50 m and bold the family's
headline: 20 m on the gold standard, 50 m on the 55-map corpus (the
`is_primary_buffer` rule of `docs/methodology/notation-key.md` § 7.1). The
difference is large, not cosmetic — the Stride A r2 ladder reads
0.6776 → 0.7027 → 0.7062 → 0.7083 at 20 m and
**0.8227 → 0.8321 → 0.8383 → 0.8419** at 50 m — and the 50 m column reproduces
`results/55map-final-board-r2-2026-09-06/final_board_50m.json` cell for cell,
which is the cross-check that it is the right column.

**A caveat step 5 must carry.** The vote threshold k is not constant down a
ladder and cannot be: a vote shell is defined relative to K. On the GS stride
ladder it runs 1 / 3 / 4 / 8 at a fixed prob_t of 0.15; on the 55-map ladders
both a carried and a rung-oracle point are committed at each rung, which is
exactly the two operating points R2 asks for. So "fixed parameters" here means
one geometry, one thinking level, one proposer temperature, one verifier, one
reference, one frame, and — at the carried point — one prob_t; k moves with K by
construction, and the review says so rather than claiming otherwise.
